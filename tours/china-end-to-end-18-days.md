## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1 · Beijing: the Imperial Axis

*Start at the center of it all. One straight line through six centuries.*

**Tian'anmen & the Forbidden City**

you start on the great square, then walk through the Meridian Gate into the palace finished in 1420. The route walks you the way the court moved: ceremonial halls first, then the residential quarters, then the imperial garden: public grandeur giving way, room by room, to private life. You'll recalibrate what the word "big" means.

**Jingshan hill**

you climb to the hilltop pavilion and turn around: below you, the golden roofs run to the horizon along Beijing's central axis: one straight line, laid down six centuries ago.

**Shichahai hutongs**

you wander courtyard lanes, lakeside streets and the small shops of old Beijing; your guide knows which lanes still live like lanes. The evening is yours around Houhai.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Beijing</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 2 · Beijing: the Great Wall at Mutianyu

*The wall, without the crush. Stand where the empire drew its line.*

**Up to the ridgeline**

the cable car or open chairlift lifts you to the ridge; the wall carries you from watchtower to watchtower while your guide reads its logic aloud: beacon towers, passes, the sightlines that made it work.

**On the brick**

you'll catch yourself running a hand along the parapet: the same gesture as the soldiers who stood here six hundred years ago.

**Lunch below the wall**

a lunch stop near the foot of the wall; your guide books it, on your account.

**Olympic Park**

back in the city, you stand between the Bird's Nest and the Water Cube: Beijing's other century, an hour from the Ming one you walked this morning.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Beijing</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 3 · Beijing: Temple of Heaven · rail to Xi'an

*The emperor's last appointment with heaven, then the fast train west.*

**Temple of Heaven**

you stand where emperors prayed for the harvest: the Hall of Prayer, the Imperial Vault, the round Circular Mound: architecture doing astronomy around you, circles for heaven, squares for earth.

**National Museum**

in one building, you pick up the backstory for everything you'll see in the next fifteen days: bronzes, ceramics, sculpture, calligraphy.

**Hands & Tea**

you watch layered soles stitched by hand at a cloth-shoe workshop, then sit down to your first proper gaiwan: how to brew, pour and hold it. Both fit around your train; then the high-speed rail carries you west, about 4.5 hours to Xi'an.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Xi'an</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 4 · Xi'an: the Terracotta Warriors

*Meet the army that waited 2,200 years. One emperor, eight thousand faces.*

**The pits**

you look down into the pits at an estimated eight thousand soldiers ranked for the first emperor: you learn to tell generals from archers by armor and hairstyle, and if you look closely at the newer excavations, you'll find traces of the original paint.

**A Shaanxi lunch stop**

the hand-pulled noodles the province is famous for; your guide books the place, on your account.

**Chinese medicine, gently**

someone reads your pulse, opens the herb drawers for you, and walks you through the logic of seasonal balance: a working introduction, not a lecture; any treatment stays optional and unhurried.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Xi'an</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 5 · Xi'an: City Wall, Sajinqiao, rail to Chengdu

*The empire ends on a bicycle. Then the road to Sichuan.*

**City Wall by bike**

you pedal the ramparts of one of China's most complete ancient walls, wide enough to ride two abreast; the full circuit is about 14 kilometers: you ride as much as suits you, walk a stretch if you'd rather. Old city on one side of you, new city on the other.

**Sajin Bridge**

you eat your way down Xi'an's Muslim culinary quarter, one street from the tourist crowds: roujiamo pressed warm into your hand, crisp bread, slow-braised pork, noodles pulled long on the counter, lamb straight off the charcoal. Your guide steers, you graze.

**Rail to Chengdu**

your evening train crosses the Qinling range, the mountains that divide China's north from its south; you're in Chengdu in about 3.5 hours.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Chengdu</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 6 · Into the mountains: Songpan

*The old trade road climbs all day: by evening you are about 2,500 meters higher than you woke up.*

**The drive north**

your van climbs out of the Chengdu Plain and into the Aba highlands in long switchbacks; you step out wherever a view or a village market earns it.

**Songpan Ancient Town**

you walk city gates, old streets and a covered bridge on the former Tea-Horse road: for centuries, tea went up to the plateau from here and horses came down, and your guide shows you where Han, Tibetan, Qiang and Hui traders kept shop in the same lanes.

**A leather workshop**

cut, stitch and stamp a small piece under a Tibetan craftsman's hands; it goes home in your pocket.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Chuanzhusi</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

*Terraced pools the color of glacier water, built by the water itself.*

**Weather honesty**

### Day 8 · Pandas & the Baima village

*The gentlest day in the mountains. A dance, a dish, a village.*

**Panda conservation center**

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

## Before you book — China, End to End FAQ

### Eighteen days: won't we burn out?

### What about altitude and weather at Huanglong?

The high stretch is exactly D6-D9, about 1,500-3,500 meters, with the highest night around 3,000 meters on D6, and you are back at Chengdu's roughly 500 meters by D9 evening.
