### 07. Terracotta Army

<p>

Set in a suburban excavated exhibition hall outside Xi'an, this site houses the underground army of Qin Shi Huang, China's first emperor—one of the country's most important archaeological discoveries. As you look down at the rows of soldiers lined up in the pits, you can see that every face is unique, the armor varies by military rank, and the formation remains clearly discernible even after more than two thousand years of burial. Your guide will help you identify which figures are officers, which are archers, and what these arrays were originally designed for. There is plenty of value in taking your time to explore: the scale is immediately impressive, but the fine details of the craftsmanship can only be truly appreciated up close.

> Essential Xi'an access to one of China's most recognized archaeological sites.

<sub>Xi'an · Pagoda</sub>

*The Tang pagoda in the Great Ci'en Temple, built under the supervision of Xuanzang to house the sutras he brought back from India.*

The Giant Wild Goose Pagoda is located within the Great Ci'en Temple at Cien Road, Yanta District, Xi'an. In the third year of the Yonghui era of the Tang Dynasty (652), the monk Xuanzang presided over the construction of the pagoda to store the sutras and Buddha statues he had brought back from India via the Silk Road. After later renovations, it became a landmark for Buddhism and sutra translation in Tang Chang'an. When you enter the temple grounds to view the pagoda and its courtyards, you must purchase tickets at that day's designated window to ascend the structure. We recommend allowing 1.5 to 3 hours for your visit. Peak season operating hours are approximately 8:00–17:30, while off-season hours are shorter, with entry closing around 17:00; please refer to the scenic area's daily announcements for current details. This brick pagoda connects Xi'an's Tang Chang'an heritage with the Silk Road's sutra-translation history, rather than focusing solely on the plaza fountains.

> A classic Tang-era landmark connected to ancient Chang'an history.

<sub>Dujiangyan · Heritage</sub>

*built more than 2,000 years ago and still working, the system manages*

<p>

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

## Before you book — Still in Use FAQ

### Is twelve days enough for four cities?

For these four, yes, and that is why there are four rather than seven. Beijing, Xi'an and Chengdu get three nights each and Shanghai gets two, so you sleep in the same bed for three nights running three times over. The moves between them are half-days on high-speed rail or one flight, not full travel days, which is what buys the arrival day with nothing on it and the easy day in Chengdu.

### Why spend a whole day going out to a waterworks?

Because it is the day that makes the other eleven hold together. The wall, the palace and the soldiers are all things that stopped: nobody defends that wall, nobody lives in that palace, nobody commands those figures. The channel at Dujiangyan was cut in the third century BC and it is still dividing the river and still watering the plain you rode past on the way in. It is also a rail day trip out and back, so your Chengdu room and your bags stay where they are.
