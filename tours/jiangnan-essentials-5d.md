## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1 · Shanghai: Arrival and Free Time to Settle In

*Day 1 is reserved for arrival, shared transfer from the station, and check-in. After completing hotel formalities, the rest of the day is free for guests to arrange as they wish.*

**Shared pickup**

Meet the local group at the designated Shanghai airport or train station, then take a shared transfer to the hotel. Please note that the shared transfer may involve waiting for other group members, with a maximum wait time of 1.5 hours.

**Check-in & Free Time**

Hotel check-in is available after 2:00 PM at locations near Hongqiao Transport Hub or Pudong Wild Animal Park, subject to final itinerary confirmation. There are no organized tours after check-in; guests are free to rest, dine, or explore at their own discretion.

<sub>• **Accommodation:** Shanghai Hongqiao Transport Hub or the Pudong Wild Animal Park area</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Full day at leisure</sub>

### Day 2 · Suzhou & Wuzhen: From Lingering Garden to Xizha Night

*Depart from Shanghai by group vehicle to Suzhou, visit a classical garden, then continue to Wuzhen. Enter Xizha in the evening, with free time to stroll at night.*

**Lingering Garden**

After meeting with your local guide, explore the Humble Administrator's Garden for about an hour, passing through its halls, corridors, framed views, and rockery spaces.

**Wuzhen Xizha**

After lunch, continue to Wuzhen and enter Xizha for about 1.5 hours; the first admission ticket is included. Walk along the waterways, stone bridges, and waterside streets to complete the main scenic route.

**Free time in the evening**

Enjoy about two hours of free time in the evening, ideal for strolling to see Xizha's night views. Wuzhen's night boat tours are not included in the package; if you wish to board, please purchase tickets on-site at your own expense.

<sub>• **Accommodation:** Outside Xizha Scenic Area, Wuzhen</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Hotel breakfast; group simple lunch; group simple dinner</sub>

### Day 3 · Nanxun and Hangzhou: Ancient Towns, Lakeshores, and Evening Shows

*In the morning, explore Nanxun's gardens and library buildings before heading to Hangzhou. In the afternoon, enjoy panoramic views of West Lake from Leifeng Pagoda, and in the evening, watch the regular Songcheng performance.*

**Nanxun Ancient Town**

Explore the ancient town of Nanxun, where the ticket includes Xiaolianzhuang (the Small Lotus Manor) and the Jiaye Library. The gardens, waterways, and library are all within a single walking route, revealing that Jiangnan's ancient towns offer more than just riverside commercial streets.

**Leifeng Pagoda**

Upon arrival in Hangzhou, visit Leifeng Pagoda to identify the West Lake's surface, causeways, and surrounding city from a higher vantage point. The climb takes about an hour and serves as a directional reference for the subsequent lakeside walk.

**West Lake Walk**

Walk along Su Causeway and Changqiao Park to enjoy close-up views of the water and distant mountains from the lakeshore. The West Lake cruise is not included; if you wish to take it, please pay on-site at your own expense.

**Songcheng Street and Eternal Love Show**

After dinner, explore Songcheng Street, then watch the regular-seat show included in the source package. The show features a grand stage, dance, and scene changes to weave together stories related to Hangzhou.

<sub>• **Accommodation:** Hangzhou</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Hotel breakfast; lunch on your own; simple group dinner</sub>

### Day 4 · Hangzhou & Shanghai: From Hefang Street to the City Skyline

*Spend the morning in Hangzhou's old town, then take an afternoon ride back to Shanghai. In the evening, overlook the Huangpu River from a high-rise observation deck, then walk into the old commercial district.*

**Hefang Street**

Spend about 1.5 hours exploring Hefang Street, Hangzhou's historic commercial street, passing by shops, alleyways, and traditional architecture. Then, return to Shanghai by group vehicle, with an estimated drive of about 2.5 hours.

**Shanghai High-Rise Observation Deck**

Depending on the final arrangement, ascend to either the 88th floor of Jin Mao Tower or the 94th floor of Shanghai World Financial Center. From the heights, identify the Huangpu River, the Bund waterfront, and the cluster of high-rises in Pudong.

**City God Temple Commercial Area**

After descending, you'll enter the old commercial district of Shanghai, a walk of about 1.5 hours. Compared to the panoramic city views from the observation deck, this stretch brings you back to street level, where you can take in the shops, alleys, and bustling crowds.

<sub>• **Accommodation:** Pudong Wild Animal Park area</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Hotel breakfast; lunch and dinner at your own expense</sub>

### Day 5 · Shanghai: The Bund, Nanjing Road, and Departure

*Spend the final morning strolling along the Huangpu River and the central shopping street. After noon, we will arrange a group transfer to the station, with no further sightseeing.*

**The Bund and Nanjing Road Pedestrian Street**

Begin with a roughly 30-minute walk along the Bund riverfront, viewing the Pudong skyline from the side of the historic buildings. Then head into Nanjing Road Pedestrian Street for about another hour.

**Unified drop-off**

After noon, the group will be transferred to the airport or train station as arranged. Different earliest departure times apply for flights from Hongqiao and Pudong; please refer to the final confirmation for booking your return transportation.

<sub>• **Accommodation:** None</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Hotel breakfast; lunch and thereafter at your own expense</sub>

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
- **Health & medications:** Bring a full supply of any prescription medication. International pharmacies exist in major cities but may not carry your brand. Travel insurance is required — we can recommend policies that cover China specifically.
- **Language:** English fluency outside hotels and tourist sites is limited. Your guides all speak English. We provide a printed pocket card with essential Chinese phrases and emergency contacts for every traveler.
