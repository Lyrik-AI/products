### 07. Terracotta Warriors

<sub>Xi'an · Heritage</sub>

*the excavation halls outside Xi'an holding the underground army of Qin Shi*

<p>
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/4Zu9RGo8.webp" alt="Terracotta Warriors" width="32%">

An unearthed exhibition hall on the outskirts of Xi'an houses the subterranean army of China's first emperor, Qin Shi Huang, and is one of the country's most significant archaeological finds. You look down upon the soldiers arrayed in the pits: each face is unique, the armor distinguished by military rank, and the formation remains distinctly legible after more than two millennia in the ground. Your guide teaches you how to tell them apart: which figures are officers, which are archers, and what these arrays were originally designed for. Why it's worth taking your time: the sheer scale is immediately apparent, while the craftsmanship details reward a closer look.

> Essential Xi'an access to one of China's most recognized archaeological sites.

## Before you book — The Middle Goes Up FAQ

### Is the altitude going to be a problem?

The high part of this route tops out at about 3,100 meters during the day and you sleep at about 2,000, which is lower than most people expect from a plateau trip and well below the height at which altitude sickness becomes common. The plan does the rest: the ascent day carries no sightseeing at all, the valley day is walked slowly with the visit closing in the early afternoon, and the descent gets its own full day. What we cannot promise is how any individual body responds, so your quote states the altitude range in writing before you pay, and if you have a heart or lung condition or have reacted to altitude before, take medical advice first. Altitude medical care, oxygen and evacuation are not included in this route's price or its insurance.

### Can I go in November or December?

Not this route, not this season. The Jiuzhaigou window closes from November through March, and the road and rail conditions up there in those months are not established for this plan, so we hold November and December closed rather than quietly swap the plateau for something else. For October to December 2026 the open window is October 8 to October 31, with the national holiday week at the start of the month excluded as well. If your dates are fixed in November or December, say so early and we will point you at a route that actually works then instead of selling you this one.

### What do I have to pay for on the day?

Three things you will actually use, and we would rather list them than let you find out at a gate. The Jiuzhaigou park shuttle, which is how the whole valley is toured. The cable car or chairlift up at Mutianyu, and the toboggan back down. All three are bought on site at present, because we have no landed price for them yet and we do not put an item in your base price before we can price it. Everything else on the included list is paid for. We are working the pricing with our partners, and if any of the three moves into the base price, your quote says so in writing before you pay.

### Fourteen days and two of them are just trains. Is that a waste?

It is the trade this route is built on, and we would make it again. Getting to the valley and back takes a travel day each way, and the ascent day is left empty on purpose so that your body has a night at 2,000 meters before the valley day rather than arriving and walking straight into it. Compressing that into a single day is the one change we decline on this route. What those two days buy you is the third one: a full day of lakes at altitude, finished early, walked slowly, with nobody chasing a train.

### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. UK, French, German, Dutch, Belgian, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Greek, Nordic, Central European, Irish, Canadian, Australian and New Zealand passports enter visa free for stays days, and we check your 14 days plus any other days in China against that window before you pay. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, so departures on or before December 17, 2026 finish inside it. US passports are different: the US is not on that list, and the 10 day transit policy does not work here either, on length and on area both, because the Jiuzhaigou section sits outside the region it allows. US guests hold a Chinese tourist or business visa, applied for in person the first time, so allow several weeks. We confirm visa status before any payment, and if the dates do not fit, we say so instead of selling.
