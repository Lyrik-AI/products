your guide reads the wall out loud, the beacon towers, the pass below, why the towers stand where they stand and what each one could see. Look along the ridge with the towers stepping down it one after another: that is the fifteen seconds worth filming.

**Coming down**

the descent is on foot unless you add the downward ride, which is not in your price. Your guide tells you what it costs on the day, or it goes onto your order in writing before you travel.

your driver takes you to the station and your Xi'an guide meets the train. The North China plain runs past the window at speed, and the city you get off in was a capital a thousand years before the one you left this morning.

### Day 5 · Eight thousand of them, in ranks

*One site, one whole day, no second stop. Today you find out what the ranks look like from three feet away.*

**The Terracotta Warriors**

the buried army made for China's first emperor, an estimated eight thousand figures, standing in the excavation halls where farmers found them. From the rail above the front rank the formation reads as one mass; then your guide points and it comes apart, a general by his armor, a crossbowman by his stance, an infantryman by the knot of his hair.

**Painted Motifs**

in the newer excavation areas the clay still carries traces of its original color. Everyone pictures this army gray. It was not. Standing over the front rank you'll notice you have dropped your voice.

**The hour**

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

That is why the plan is built with roughly a full day of margin inside the window, and why we compute your hours from your actual arrival time rather than from a schedule. What we cannot promise is a delay of any size: airlines and immigration halls are not ours. If a long delay compresses your window, the day-ten content is cut from the Shenzhen end and the crossing time stays where it is, because the crossing is the one thing that cannot move. What you still get in that case is the same guide, the same vehicle and an exit with its four-hour buffer intact.

### Four cities in ten days with three moves. Is that too fast?

It is the tightest plan we run on this window, and we would rather say that than have you find out on day seven. Here is how it is arranged: the first six days do not move at all, three nights in Beijing and three in Xi'an, so the two heaviest sites each get a whole day to themselves. All three transfers sit in the last four days, and day seven is deliberately light, one morning walk and an afternoon flight. If that still reads as too fast, we run a three-city version of this same window that trades one city for two calmer days. Tell us which you want and we will lay both against your dates.

### Can we ride in a driverless taxi in Shenzhen?

Sometimes, and we will not pretend it is ours to promise. These vehicles run on a commercial operator's routes and the operator decides day by day what is running, so the ride is not in your price and it is not part of what this plan sells you. On day ten we check availability for your date; where it runs, we arrange it and state its price in writing when you choose it. Where it does not, the day continues with a Shenzhen alternative that is written into your quote before you travel. Either way day ten keeps what it is built on: your vehicle and guide all day, Huaqiangbei read floor by floor, and four hours of buffer before the crossing.

### Is the crossing into Hong Kong included?

No, and that is also why this route is not open for booking yet. The plan ends at the mainland exit port: your driver takes you there and your guide walks you to the immigration hall, with at least four hours of buffer held ahead of your planned crossing.
