## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.

## Before you book — One Country, Three Clocks FAQ

### I hold a US passport. Do I need a visa for this?

Not necessarily, and that is what this route is built around. The US is not on the 30-day visa-free list, so you run it either on the 10-day visa-free transit policy or on a Chinese tourist or business visa, including a 10-year multiple-entry one. The transit path is why the plan is 9 days and why it leaves the mainland at a land crossing into Hong Kong: it needs a confirmed onward ticket with date and seat, held before you enter, and every activity has to stay inside the areas the policy permits, which Beijing, Xi'an and Shenzhen all do. Before any payment we compute your actual landing and exit times against the window and fix the path in writing. If your flights do not leave enough margin, we say so instead of selling.

### Why do we walk out at a land border instead of just flying home?

Because the exit is what makes the 9 days work. The transit policy is counted from entry to exit and it needs you to leave the mainland for a third country or region inside the window. Shenzhen's Futian crossing puts Hong Kong about 30 minutes away by high-speed rail, which is the shortest and most predictable exit on the map for a plan that enters at Beijing. Our vehicle takes you from the hotel to the crossing and your guide sees you to the departure hall. From there Hong Kong is yours: hotel, meals and your international flight home, booked for that evening or later.

### Is the driverless taxi guaranteed?

No, and we will not pretend otherwise. It runs on a commercial operator's live service lines, so the operator decides whether it runs on your date. We book it by date before you arrive and reconfirm it after you land. Where it is not running, that time goes to a longer walk through Huaqiangbei with your guide and your price does not change, and you are told which of the two you are getting before you pay. What holds either way: the day is about how this city makes things, and the market and the exhibition carry that on their own. The ride is a bonus, never the reason to come.

### Nine days and three cities. Are we just moving the whole time?

There are exactly two travel legs in nine days: a train on the afternoon of day 4 and a flight on the afternoon of day 7. Each takes half a day and neither is stacked on top of a full day of sightseeing. You get three nights in Beijing, three in Xi'an and two in Shenzhen, so you unpack three times rather than six. Day 1 carries no sightseeing at all, and day 9 is one exhibition and the border. The one dense day is day 4, and we flag it rather than bury it.

### What is October to December actually like on this route?

Beijing and Xi'an are dry and clear through autumn and cold by December, so this is the season the wall and the wall-top ride are most comfortable to be outdoors on; Shenzhen stays mild the whole window. Two things follow the calendar and we put both in writing before you pay: the Mutianyu uphill cable car schedule is confirmed with the operator for November and December dates, and cold or wind on the Xi'an wall turns the bicycle ride into a walk along the same stretch. We also do not depart between October 1 and 7, the national holiday week, because the crowds at the Terracotta Warriors that week change what you get for your day.
