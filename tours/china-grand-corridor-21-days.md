### Day 17 · The cliffside trail of Tianmen Mountain

*A long cableway out of town, then walkways cut into a cliff face. Today is the vertical one.*

**Tianmen Mountain cableway**

a passenger cableway 7.4 km long, one of the longest of its kind, boarding at the edge of the city and climbing to the mountain top; the return ride is in your price. You leave from a street with traffic on it and arrive on a ridge, without a road in between.

**Cliff walkway**

the walkways are cut into the rock face with glass-floored sections built out over the drop. A solid-floor path runs beside every glass section, so anyone who would rather not walk on glass walks the same route on stone.

**Heaven's Gate**

the opening straight through the rock, left by a cliff collapse recorded in AD 263. A stairway climbs to it from the mountain road; the stairway is the one genuinely steep piece of walking on this route and climbing it is your choice, since the arch reads just as well from the bottom.

**Evening lighting program**

the mountain runs an evening lighting program on some dates. When it is operating on your confirmed date we go, and it carries no extra charge; when it is not, the evening is yours and the day has already stood on the cableway and the walkway.

### Day 18 · From Tianzi Mountain to Jinbian Stream

*The last full day of stone, and the one that changes elevation most. It ends at a loom.*

**Tianzi Mountain**

the high terrace over the densest part of the pillar forest, where the columns stand in ranks below you rather than around you. The mountain is named after a Tujia leader who styled himself Son of Heaven, which tells you who held this country before the maps did.

**Golden Whip Stream**

down to the valley floor and along the streambed, the same columns seen from underneath with the canopy closing overhead. Walking length is set on the day to suit your party. Wild macaques work this stretch and will take food out of an open hand, so food stays packed and your guide walks the party through. Down here you'll find yourself lowering your voice without deciding to.

**Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon glass bridge**

a glass-decked bridge spanning 430 meters across the canyon; when it opened in 2016 it was the longest and highest span of its kind. A solid-floor path runs alongside for anyone who would rather cross on something opaque.

**Tujia brocade workshop**

xilankapu, literally "the flowered bedcover," is the local Tujia weaving tradition, worked on a waist loom with the pattern counted in threads rather than drawn on paper. You get hands on the loom when the workshop is open on your date, and a visit without the hands-on when it is not.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

## Before you book — River First, Then Stone FAQ

### Twenty-one days is a long trip. Will it drag?

It is built so that it does not. One idea runs through the whole route, walls then water then stone, and each day carries one piece of it rather than three unrelated sights. Three days are deliberately easy, on days eight, fourteen and twenty, and you unpack once for four nights in Chengdu and again for four in Zhangjiajie. Twenty-one days could hold nine hotel changes; this plan uses six.

### You say the Yangtze days are not sold. So what am I actually buying?

Right now, nothing: this route is not open for booking at all, and that is the honest answer. Three things have to be true first, a cabin held on a named sailing, the cabin grade chosen and written into the inclusions, and the operator confirming in writing the English-language service on board. We will not take a deposit against a segment we have not bought. What is already settled is the other seventeen days: the six cities with their guides and vehicles, the rail and air sectors, and the tickets named one by one in the inclusions above. Tell us your dates and we write to you the day the river segment lands.

### I am not good with heights. Does Zhangjiajie rule me out?

No. There are exactly two glass sections on this route, the Tianmen cliff walkway on day seventeen and the Grand Canyon glass bridge on day eighteen, and each has a solid-floor path running beside it that goes to the same places. The Bailong elevator and the Tianmen cableway are enclosed cabins. The one steep climb, the stairway up to Heaven's Gate, is optional and the arch reads just as well from the bottom. Tell your guide on day one and the whole Zhangjiajie block is walked on stone.

### Why can we only start on a Monday?

Because the sailing this route hangs on runs once a week, and everything before and after it is timed to reach the pier on the right day. A Monday start has a second benefit: day two lands on a Tuesday, which keeps the Forbidden City morning off the palace's Monday closing day. If Monday does not work for you, the river segment stops fitting and we will design you a different plan rather than bend this one and hope.

### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. UK and most European passports enter visa-free for stays days. Canadian, Australian and New Zealand passports do the same. Before you pay we check your 21 days plus any other days in China against that window. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, so a departure on or before December 11, 2026 finishes inside it, and every date we offer does. US passports are different: the US is not on that list and 21 days is more than double what the 10-day transit policy allows, so every US guest holds a Chinese tourist or business visa, with the fee and the appointment their own. We confirm visa status before any payment, and if the dates do not fit we say so instead of selling.
