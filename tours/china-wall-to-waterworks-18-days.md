## Overview

China spent two thousand years building walls, and just as long constructing the roads and water projects that spanned them. Beijing has two walls: one encircling the imperial city, the other stretching along the mountain ridge. In Xi'an, you can cycle along the ancient city wall before boarding a high-speed train toward the southwest. The Dujiangyan irrigation system outside Chengdu has been diverting water since the 3rd century BC, and it still functions today. Then follow the Yangtze eastward to conclude the journey on the banks of Shanghai's Huangpu River. From walls to water, each stop picks up the question raised by the last.

### Day 3 · The wall, without the climb

### Day 6 · Eight thousand, for one man

*An estimated eight thousand figures, buried so that one emperor would not have to arrive anywhere alone. Today is about what one person's power looked like when it was total.*

**The Terracotta Warriors**

you walk the pits in the order they were opened, and your guide teaches you to read rank before you look at the crowd: armor plates, the tie of the hair, the stance of the hands that once held a weapon. Generals, crossbowmen, foot soldiers. In the more recently excavated areas, traces of the original paint are still there on the surface.

**The one to stand in front of**

find a figure in the second row with its face intact and stay there a minute. No two were finished the same way, and once you have seen that, the pit stops being a crowd.

**Crowds**

### Day 7 · The wall you get to be on top of

*Beijing's walls kept you outside. This one you ride around, at your own speed, for as long as you want.*

**Xi'an City Wall by bike**

you take a bicycle at the gate and ride the loop on top of the wall, wide enough here for two bikes side by side. Inside the loop: rooftops, courtyards, the drum tower. Outside: the modern city carrying on. Corner towers give you somewhere to stop and put a foot down. Admission and the bike are both in your base price. Somewhere on the far side you'll notice you have stopped talking and are just riding.

**Three walls, one thought**

this is the third wall in seven days. The palace wall shut people out of a household, the Great Wall shut horses out of a country, and this one holds a city that is still living inside it. Your guide leaves you to notice the difference rather than announcing it.

**Bell and Drum Tower area with the Muslim Quarter**

in the evening you walk the two towers that used to keep the city's time, then the lanes behind them where Xi'an's Hui community trades and cooks: bread stamped and baked against the wall of the oven, whole spices in open sacks, meat turned over coals. ### Day 10 · A river split in two, and still split

*The oldest working thing you will stand on in China, and the center of this whole route. You go out and come back the same day.*

**Dujiangyan irrigation works**

an hour out of Chengdu, an engineer facing a river that flooded the plain every year decided not to dam it. Instead the channel was shaped so the Min River splits itself: an inner stream that waters the Chengdu plain, an outer stream that carries the flood away. That was the 3rd century BC. You walk out over the divide and watch the two flows separate under your feet, and the plain behind you is still being watered by the inner one this morning. Standing on the divide you'll find you have gone quiet, because it takes a minute to accept how old this is.

**A small Taoist temple on Mount Qingcheng**

an hour further on, one of the principal Taoist mountains in China: steps under old trees, mist that does not lift. You go to a small working temple away from the main visitor route, sit with a resident priest, and take a taiji session on the temple ground. The forms are slow on purpose, which after ten days of walking is the point.

**Back to Chengdu**

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

## Before you book — Walls First, Then Water FAQ

### Is the Yangtze part actually happening?

Not yet, and we will not pretend otherwise. The ship, the Chongqing ground services and the Yichang link are three separate purchases and none of them is made, so days 12 to 16 are printed in the itinerary but sold to no one: not in the highlights, not in the inclusions, not in any price. That is also why the route takes no deposits at all right now. What you can do today is tell us your dates and we will write to you when those three are contracted. If you would rather travel this season than wait, a shorter version that runs Beijing, Xi'an, Chengdu and Dujiangyan and stops there avoids the unbought stretch entirely, and it carries the same argument from the wall to the water. We can plan that against your dates.

### Why is Dujiangyan the center of this route instead of a side trip?

Because it is the point the whole eighteen days turns on. Everything before it was built to divide: a wall around a palace, a wall along a ridge, a wall around a city. Dujiangyan is the opposite decision, taken in the 3rd century BC, and it is still running: the river is split by the shape of its own bed into a stream that waters the Chengdu plain and a stream that carries the flood away. Stand on the divide and the previous nine days rearrange themselves. Most itineraries give it ninety minutes on the way to somewhere; this one gives it a whole day with Mount Qingcheng, and comes back to sleep in the same Chengdu room.

### Eighteen days is long. Will we be worn out by the middle?

The middle is where the plan slows down on purpose. Day 9 is a panda morning and a teahouse afternoon in one city with no packing; day 10 goes out to the waterworks and comes back to the same room; the Chengdu nights all run in one hotel. Climbing is done by machine where machines exist: the cable car up the Great Wall, the toboggan down, and a bicycle instead of your feet on the Xi'an wall. Where a site is big, the route is cut to one line with places to sit, which is why the palace day carries no second monument.

### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. Stays of days are visa-free for the UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand. The same window covers France, Germany, the Netherlands and Belgium. It covers the Nordic countries, Central Europe and Ireland. It covers Italy, Spain, Portugal and Greece. We check your 18 days plus any extra days in China against that window before you pay. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, so a departure on or before December 14, 2026 finishes inside it. US passports are different: the US is not on that list and 18 days is far longer than the ten-day transit policy allows, so every US guest holds a Chinese tourist or business visa, applied for in person the first time. We confirm visa status before any payment, and if your dates do not fit the window, we say so instead of selling.

### October to December: what are the crowds and the weather doing?

The first week of October is the national holiday week and both the Terracotta Warriors and the panda base run at their yearly peak then, so departure dates are set to keep days 6 and 9 out of it; October 8, 2026 is the first sellable date this season. After that week the north is dry and cool and Sichuan turns gray and damp, which suits a teahouse afternoon better than a bright one would. On the river these are the low-water months, and operators adjust sailings and the stretch actually cruised in that season, which is one more reason no date is opened before the operator's own calendar for that season is in hand.
