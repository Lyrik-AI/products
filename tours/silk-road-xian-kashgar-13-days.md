## Things to do

### 01. Terracotta Warriors

<sub>Xi'an · Heritage</sub>

*the excavation halls outside Xi'an holding the underground army of Qin Shi*

<p>
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/4Zu9RGo8.webp" alt="Terracotta Warriors" width="32%">
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/70bc50b897e9.jpg" alt="Terracotta Warriors" width="32%">
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/788c1d294602.jpg" alt="Terracotta Warriors" width="32%">
</p>

On the outskirts of Xi'an, this excavation hall houses the underground army of Qin Shi Huang, China's first emperor—one of the country's most significant archaeological finds. As you look down at the ranks of soldiers lining the pits, each face is unique, armor varies by military rank, and even after more than two thousand years buried, the formations remain clearly discernible. Your guide helps you identify who the officers are, who the archers are, and what these formations were originally designed for. Why take your time: the sheer scale can be grasped at a glance, but the fine craftsmanship requires getting close up to truly appreciate.

> Essential Xi'an access to one of China's most recognized archaeological sites.

### 03. Xi'an Ancient City Wall Cycling

<sub>Xi'an · Cycling</sub>

*one of the most complete surviving ancient city walls in China, wide enough on top to cycle two abreast, still ringed around the old center. Admission and bike hire are in the base price.*

<p>
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/ggD7rC8G.webp" alt="Xi&#x27;an Ancient City Wall Cycling" width="32%">
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/daf832e303d0.jpg" alt="Xi&#x27;an Ancient City Wall Cycling" width="32%">
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/8f7c7a922b33.jpg" alt="Xi&#x27;an Ancient City Wall Cycling" width="32%">
</p>

Climb on and ride—beneath your wheels, old grey brick; on one side, the historic layout inside the walls; on the other, the modern new city beyond. The full loop is about 14 km, and you can ride as far as you like: the entire circuit, just a section, or switch to walking. You set the pace: each gate has a ramp, the path is paved in brick, and no motor vehicles are permitted on top of the wall. The included rental time and the basis for any excess-fee billing are set out in writing in your quote. This is the longest unsheltered stretch of outdoor time on this route; if the weather or physical conditions warrant it, the ride will change to a walk on the ramparts. For November and December departures, the way we handle the low temperatures will be spelled out in the quote. It is also the most direct lesson on Chinese urban planning on this trip—inside the walls, ancient streets and alleys; outside, a skyline of high-rise towers in the new city.

> Active heritage experience on one of China's best-preserved city walls.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
- **Health & medications:** Bring a full supply of any prescription medication. International pharmacies exist in major cities but may not carry your brand. Travel insurance is required — we can recommend policies that cover China specifically.
- **Language:** English fluency outside hotels and tourist sites is limited. Your guides all speak English. We provide a printed pocket card with essential Chinese phrases and emergency contacts for every traveler.

### Why does my departure date have to be a particular day of the week?

Because the Kashgar bazaar meets on Sundays, and we put it on day eleven. Everything else counts back from there. The first open departures are October 8, 15, 22 and 29. ### Can you guarantee we get into the Mogao Caves?

Not yet, and we would rather say so than sell it. Three of the thirteen days are travel days, and each of them is given the whole day rather than squeezed around a sight.
