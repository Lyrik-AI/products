## Overview

This 21-day itinerary prioritizes time in cities over simply adding more stops. Each stay lasts four nights: Beijing invites you to wander its hutongs and learn gaiwan tea; Xi’an has you riding the city wall and exploring Sajin Bridge; and Chengdu sets aside an entire afternoon at a teahouse in People’s Park. Three high-speed rail segments connect Beijing, Xi’an, Chengdu, and Chongqing. The riverboat leg from Chongqing to Yichang—sailing dates, cabin class, and pricing—will be confirmed based on your travel dates. Upon arriving in Yichang, you’ll fly to Shanghai.

### 10. Terracotta Army

<sub>Xi'an · Heritage</sub>

*the excavation halls outside Xi'an holding the underground army of Qin Shi*

<p>
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/4Zu9RGo8.webp" alt="Terracotta Army" width="32%">
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/70bc50b897e9.jpg" alt="Terracotta Army" width="32%">

</p>

The excavation hall on the outskirts of Xi'an houses the underground army of Qin Shi Huang, China’s first emperor, and one of the country’s most significant archaeological discoveries. From above, you look down at the soldiers arrayed in the pits: each face is unique, the armor varies by military rank, and the formations remain distinct even after more than two millennia of burial. Your guide shows you how to distinguish the officers from the archers and explains what these arrays were originally intended for. Why it’s worth lingering: the scale is immediately striking, but the craftsmanship can only be truly appreciated up close.

> Essential Xi'an access to one of China's most recognized archaeological sites.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
- **Health & medications:** Bring a full supply of any prescription medication. International pharmacies exist in major cities but may not carry your brand. Travel insurance is required — we can recommend policies that cover China specifically.

## Before you book — The Fourth Night FAQ

### Twenty-one days and only six places. What do we actually do on the fourth day in a city? 21

The things the guidebooks stop listing. In Beijing it is a lane walk, a tea table and a cloisonné bench; in Xi'an it is two hours riding around the top of the city wall and then eating one street over from the tourist flow; in Chengdu it is a whole afternoon in a park teahouse with nothing after it; in Shanghai it is a corner of People's Park where parents look for matches for their grown children. None of those are things to see. They are what the city is doing while you are there.

### What is the situation with the Yangtze part?

Days 14 to 17 are a four-day, three-night downstream sailing from Chongqing to Yichang, sold by the operator on an all-inclusive ticket. We have not secured a cabin block: the operator holds no space and takes named bookings first come first served through its own system, so we cannot promise you a cabin today. That is why the ship is not in the price, not in the inclusions and not sold to you as a highlight, and why we take no orders and no deposits on this route yet. What holds either way: the 17 land nights and the four-night cities, the guides, the three rail legs and the flight. All of that is arranged, and the pier transfers at both ends are ours. Tell us your dates and we write to you the day a cabin is held.

### Can we go in December?

Not on this route. The sailing this plan is built on leaves Chongqing on Fridays and its 2026 season ends on November 27, and no December sailing fits these 21 days. The last departure of this selling season is Saturday, November 14, 2026, finishing on December 4. ### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. UK and European passports enter visa-free for stays days, and so do Canadian, Australian and New Zealand ones. Your 21 days plus any extra days in China are checked against that window order by order. Every departure in this selling season finishes on or before December 4, 2026, inside the current policy. Because the sailing date is fixed, we confirm processing time against your departure date in writing before any payment. If the dates do not fit, we say so instead of selling.

### How much walking is there really?

Three days carry real distance: the Forbidden City half day, the Mutianyu wall walk, and the Chongqing morning through the city's levels. Every climb on this route is done by machine: cable car up at Mutianyu, a seated sled down, lifts and escalators through Chongqing. The Xi'an wall is ridden, not walked. Four of the 21 days are rest days, three of those are on the river and ask nothing of anyone, and the two remaining land rest days have a monastery and an evening food ride on them.
