### Day 7 · Pandas First, No Rush After

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
- **Health & medications:** Bring a full supply of any prescription medication. International pharmacies exist in major cities but may not carry your brand. Travel insurance is required — we can recommend policies that cover China specifically.

### Four nights at 3,650 meters. Am I going to be all right?

Nobody can promise you a body's response to altitude, and we do not. What we can do is design against it, and this route is mostly that design. Eight days at low altitude come first. Altitude is gained in one direction and never out of order. The flight up gets a whole day of its own. The first full day on the plateau is left completely empty. The highest ground of the trip waits until after seventy-two hours. Pulse-oximeter monitoring runs alongside the group. Rooms up there must have in-room oxygen supply, and that requirement is precisely why this route is not yet on sale. Before any order you complete a written screening covering cardiopulmonary history and previous altitude reaction. If the answers say this is not your trip, we will tell you so. The low half still gives you the palace axis, the wall, the buried army and a teahouse afternoon.

### Why is the departure window only ten days long?

Because the calendar closes it from both ends. We do not sell the plateau leg from November through March, so all 15 days have to finish inside October, which puts the last departure on October 17. And we hold no departures between October 1 and 7, the national holiday week, when crowds at the Terracotta Warriors peak and a full day at the pits stops being a full day at the pits. That leaves October 8 to 17. If your dates fall outside it, we would rather decline than improvise a version of this route that does not work.

### Do I need a visa, and what is a Tibet permit?

Most guests on this route need no visa. Most European passports enter China visa-free for stays days. UK, Canadian, Australian and New Zealand ones do too. Before you pay we check your 15 days plus any other days in China against that window. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, and the October window sits inside it. The Tibet travel permit is separate from a visa: it is a document required for the Tibet Autonomous Region, pre-reviewed at least 15 days before departure with no expedited channel, which is why we stop taking a date 20 days out. Here is the part we will not gloss: whether a guest entering visa-free can obtain that permit has no written answer on file yet, and we will not confirm an order on the assumption that it can. US passports are a separate case again, and we are not quoting US enquiries while that decision sits with our commercial review.

### Fifteen days and five cities. How much of it is spent traveling?

Four days carry a long transfer, and we gave each of them the room to be a transfer rather than squeezing sights around it. Two are by high-speed rail with a sightseeing morning before them: hutong lanes before the Beijing to Xi'an train, the city wall before the Xi'an to Chengdu train, both city center to city center. The two air segments, up to the plateau and back down, take a whole day each and carry nothing else, which is a decision about altitude rather than about logistics. That leaves nine days on the ground in five places, plus the empty acclimatization day. Your own vehicle runs both ends of every transfer, and your guide hands you over at the door.
