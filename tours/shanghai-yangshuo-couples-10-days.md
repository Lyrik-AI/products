## Overview

A wedding is ten months of laboring for others. This plan aims to hand that burden to something else—here, a river. Shanghai takes up the first three days, since you have both just flown a long way and a city can be tiring. A garden, plane-tree streets, and the water's edge at night. Then the next morning, look at the same water from the tower; at dusk, look at it once more from the commuter ferry. After that, the schedule loosens. You fly to Guilin, sleep one night, and the next afternoon a boat carries you out of one city and into another with no roads connecting them. For the next four nights, in the same room in Yangshuo: bamboo rafts, sunrises, caves, and the riverbank in the twilight. One afternoon is devoted only to drinking tea. On the ninth day, you fly back to Shanghai for the final night, because Yangshuo has no airport, and we prefer that last night to be a rounded ending rather than a rush. At some point on the river in the afternoon, you will notice that the two of you have not spoken for twenty minutes—and that is quite all right.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
- **Health & medications:** Bring a full supply of any prescription medication. International pharmacies exist in major cities but may not carry your brand. Travel insurance is required — we can recommend policies that cover China specifically.
- **Language:** English fluency outside hotels and tourist sites is limited. Your guides all speak English. We provide a printed pocket card with essential Chinese phrases and emergency contacts for every traveler.

## Before you book — The Water Sets the Clock FAQ

### Can you arrange a photographer and set up the ceremony moments?

Not on this version, and we will not pretend otherwise. Tell us your dates and we will write to you the day the three are real.

### Four and a half hours on a boat. Isn't that a long time?

It is long, and that is the point of day five. There is no other water link between Guilin and Yangshuo, so the choice is this boat or a car on a road, and the car saves you three hours you have nowhere else to spend. On board you sit, you eat something, you stop naming the peaks after the first hour. It is also the only leg of the trip where neither of you has anything to do. The boat class and deck for your order are written into your quote, and the sailing time for your dates is confirmed before you pay.

### Do we have to get up in the dark?

Once, on day seven, and only if you want to. The Xianggong Hill sunrise leaves before first light, it is the one start earlier than 8:00 on the whole route, and you are told so in writing before you order. You can decline it on the order: day seven then starts late and runs to Xingping Ancient Town instead, at the same price. If you keep it and the morning comes in wet or misty, there is no color from the viewpoint and we will not claim otherwise; four nights in one hotel means the guide and vehicle are still yours to try the next morning, with the cave and the sunset moved back half a day.

### Why fly back to Shanghai for one night instead of going home from Guilin?

Because Yangshuo has no airport and no port of entry, so every version of this trip goes back through Guilin anyway. From there you either connect through another airport on the day you fly home, or you sleep one night in the city you started in and leave from a port you already know. We chose the second: day nine is a boat town in the morning and a hotel bed in Shanghai by evening, and day ten is a breakfast walk on the plane-tree street from day two, then the airport. Both internal flights and the road transfer to Guilin airport are in the base price.

### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. UK, most European and Canadian passports enter visa-free for stays days, and so do Australian and New Zealand passports. Before you pay we check your 10 days plus any extra days in China against that window. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, and this version sells October departures, which finish well inside it. US passports are the exception: this route is not offered on a US passport at present, because Shanghai in and Shanghai out on a US round trip does not form the onward third-country leg the ten-day visa-free transit policy requires, and ten days leaves that policy no margin. We would rather tell you that than sell you a trip you cannot legally take.
