## Overview

One country, six cities, each with its own rhythm. Beijing preserves the floor plan of its imperial palace; you walk along one of its central axes. Xi'an still wears the city walls it once used for defense; you ride atop them. Chengdu invites you to sit down, so the journey deliberately slows in the middle. Shanghai places trade on both banks of the river, while Shenzhen arranges electronic components and finished products across different floors of its markets. Ending in Guangzhou with tea and a walk by the Pearl River, you can finally describe the distinct character of each city.

### 09. Giant Wild Goose Pagoda

<sub>Xi'an · Pagoda</sub>

*The Tang pagoda in the Great Ci'en Temple, built under the supervision of Xuanzang to house the sutras he brought back from India.*

<p>
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/2be95bfc5748.jpg" alt="Giant Wild Goose Pagoda" width="32%">

The Giant Wild Goose Pagoda stands within the Grand Compassion Temple (Daci'en Temple) on Ci'en Road in Xi'an's Yanta District. In 652 (the third year of Yonghui in the Tang Dynasty), Xuanzang led the construction of the pagoda to house the scriptures and Buddhist statues he brought back from India via the Silk Road. After subsequent renovations, it became Tang Chang'an's definitive landmark for Buddhism and sutra translation. When visiting, you can explore the temple courtyard and pagoda structure; tickets to climb the pagoda are sold on-site according to the day's designated hours. We recommend allowing 1.5–3 hours; during peak season, the site is typically open from 8:00 AM to 5:30 PM, with shorter hours in the off-season and entry closing around 5:00 PM, subject to the official daily schedule. The pagoda connects Tang-era Chang'an and the Silk Road history of Buddhist translation in a single brick structure, offering far more than just the plaza's fountains.

> A classic Tang-era landmark connected to ancient Chang'an history.

<sub>Dujiangyan · Heritage</sub>

*built more than 2,000 years ago and still working, the system manages*

<p>
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/9d9a43ba2c4d.jpg" alt="Dujiangyan" width="32%">

Built more than two millennia ago and still in use today, this water management system regulates the Min River without a conventional dam. You can stroll along its main channel and watch the water be split in two: floodwaters are diverted down one path, while irrigation water flows along the other, nourishing the entire Chengdu Plain. Simply put, its importance is this: every meal you eat this week begins with these waters.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1 · Arrive in Beijing; start with a relaxing rest

*You land in Beijing and the day asks nothing of you. The trip starts tomorrow, at the gate of a palace.*

**Arrival**

your flight comes into Beijing and tonight's hotel is already in your plan, the first of thirteen nights. Nothing is scheduled and nothing needs to be.

**Getting to the hotel**

the arrival transfer is not part of the base price on this route. Whether your order carries an airport pickup is written into your quote before you pay, and if it does not, your guide sends you the taxi and metro instructions for your terminal in advance.

**Evening**

nothing is scheduled. Your guide is reachable, and a place to eat near the hotel is recommended and booked for you on request, on your own account.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Beijing</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** None included today</sub>

### Day 2 · One axis, then the hill that looks back

*You walk the palace along a single line and come out the back. Then you climb the hill facing that gate and see what you just walked through.*

**The Forbidden City**

your reservation is timed, so the morning starts at a fixed hour. You take the ceremonial halls first, then the living quarters behind them, then the imperial garden, and your guide keeps the route on that one line instead of chasing side courts, with places to sit along the way.

**Out through the north gate**

you leave by the gate at the back of the palace, and the hill is straight across the street. The crossing is short enough that most people are still talking about the last hall when they start climbing.

**Jingshan Park**

stairs to the pavilion at the top, and from up there the roofs you have been walking between for three hours line up in rows with the Beijing Central Axis running through the middle. You will find yourself pointing rather than talking.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Beijing</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 3 · The wall, a whole day of it

*The drive out is long, so the wall gets the day to itself. Read the wall instead of photographing it and leaving.*

**The road out**

Head north from central Beijing into the mountains; allow ample round-trip travel time. To keep the day relaxed, only the Mutianyu Great Wall is scheduled, with no additional stops.

**Mutianyu Great Wall**

a restored run of wall along the ridge with watchtowers you can walk between. The climb to the wall is on foot unless you have added the cable car or chairlift to your order, and your guide sets the pace to the slowest walker in your party.

**On the wall**

you stand in a tower doorway and your guide reads the ground for you, where the beacon towers are, where the pass runs below, and why the wall turns where it turns. The ridge line goes on past where the restored section ends.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Beijing</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 4 · The square, the altar, the bronzes, then a train

*Beijing's public face in the morning, its shapes and its collection after. Leave the capital by rail, at the speed the country moves at now.*

**Tian'anmen Square**

everyone goes through security screening to enter, and the queue is budgeted separately so the rest of the day does not slide. You walk the square itself, which is where most of China's twentieth century happened.

**The Temple of Heaven**

three structures on one line inside a walled park that the neighborhood still uses as a park. Your guide gives you the shape rule first, round for heaven and square for earth, and after that you can read the compound yourself.

**The National Museum of China**

bronzes, ceramics, sculpture and calligraphy, by timed reservation. Two hours here puts dates on the things you have been walking past outdoors for three days.

**High-speed rail to Xi'an**

your guide walks you to the platform and hands you over at the door. The rail class for your order is written into your quote, and a guide meets your train at the Xi'an end.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Xi'an</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 5 · Eight thousand faces, one at a time

*The whole day goes to one site, and it needs it. Learn to tell the ranks apart and the pits stop being a crowd.*

**Out to the pits**

about an hour on the road from the city, and your guide uses it to give you the ranks before you see them, so the first pit is not just a wall of soldiers.

**The Terracotta Army**

you work through the pits one by one. Armor and hairstyle tell the generals from the crossbowmen from the infantry, and in the newer excavation areas you can still see traces of the original paint on the figures. The site holds an estimated eight thousand figures.

**Standing at the rail**

the front ranks are lit and the hall runs back into shadow, rank behind rank, further than the photographs ever show. You will lower your voice without deciding to.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Xi'an</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 6 · On top of the wall, then behind it

*Ride the old city's outline in daylight and walk its food lanes after dark. One city at two hours of the day.*

**Xi'an City Wall**

you go up at a gate tower and take a rented bicycle along the top, on flat brick wide enough to ride two abreast. The wall closes a full loop around the old center, so you can turn back whenever you like and still have ridden a side of the city.

**From the parapet**

on one side the lanes of the old town run away below you, on the other the towers of the new city stand behind the wall line. It is the clearest picture of the whole trip's argument, and it is free.

**The Bell and Drum Tower quarter and the Muslim Quarter**

after dark, on foot with your guide. The two towers used to keep the city's time; the lanes behind them do the eating. Your guide tells you what each stall is selling before you buy anything, and what you buy is on your own account.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Xi'an</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 7 · A pagoda, then the mountains

*A short morning before a long ride south. Cross the range that separates the two halves of China and arrive somewhere that eats differently.*

**Giant Wild Goose Pagoda**

the brick pagoda and the temple compound around it, an hour and a half in the morning while the quarter is still quiet.

**High-speed rail through the Qinling**

the line south runs through the Qinling mountains, the range that separates northern China from the south, most of it in tunnel and viaduct. You get off in a city that grows tea, not wheat.

**Into Chengdu**

a guide meets your train and you are at the hotel with the evening still in front of you. The next two days are the slowest of the fourteen, by design.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Chengdu</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 8 · Pandas while they are moving, then nowhere to be

*The morning is the only hour the pandas are worth the trip. The afternoon has no task in it at all.*

**Chengdu panda base**

you go early, because that is when they climb, eat and fight over the same length of bamboo. By mid-afternoon they are asleep, which is why this is not an afternoon item.

**People's Park teahouse**

bamboo chairs under the trees, a gaiwan each, and the man with the kettle comes back as long as you are sitting there. Cards at the next table, the ear cleaner working the row behind you with his tuning fork.

**The afternoon**

nothing follows it. Your guide stays if you want the conversation and steps back if you do not. You will notice at some point that nobody has looked at a phone for an hour.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Chengdu</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 9 · A day trip to the works that waters this plain

*Out by rail in the morning, back to the same hotel at night. See what twenty-three centuries of maintenance looks like.*

**Rail out to Dujiangyan**

Drive from Chengdu to Dujiangyan, then return to the same Chengdu hotel for the night. This is one of the busiest days of the trip, framed by a more relaxed pace on either side.

**Dujiangyan irrigation works**

Standing at Dujiangyan's water-dividing point, you watch the Minjiang River branch into different channels. There is no transverse dam here; your guide will explain how the Fish Mouth, Flying Sand Weir, and Treasure Bottle Mouth each serve their purpose.

**A small Taoist temple on Mount Qingcheng**

a working temple away from the front-gate crowds. The resident priest sits down with you and takes your questions, and then there is tai chi on the temple ground, done slowly enough that nobody has to be good at it.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Chengdu</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 10 · Three lanes, a mask, and a plane

*Chengdu's shopfront in the morning and its neighborhood theater after lunch. Leave the city after the last face has changed.*

**Kuanzhai Alley and Taikoo Li**

Start with the three courtyard alleys of Kuanzhai Alley, then explore the modern district of Taikoo Li. Today, Kuanzhai Alley is a commercial street filled with teahouses and street food stalls, and your guide will provide an honest account of its current state.

**Sichuan opera, community theater**

you go backstage before the show and watch the faces go on. Then face-changing from close up, near enough that you still cannot see how it happens.

**Flight to Shanghai**

your guide takes you to the airport after the theater. The cabin for your order is written into your quote, and a guide meets you in Shanghai.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Shanghai</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 11 · A garden, a collection, and two banks

*Start in the oldest quarter and end at the water after dark. Watch a city look at itself across a river.*

**Yu Garden and the old city**

pavilions, rockery, ponds and winding paths inside the oldest quarter of the city. It comes first because everything else you will see today was built to answer it.

**Shanghai Museum**

bronzes, ceramics, painting and calligraphy, on a timed entry. Two hours, and your guide picks a route through it rather than starting at gallery one.

**The Bund after dark**

the old waterfront buildings behind you, lit; the Pudong towers across the water, also lit; the ferries and the barges going between them. Your guide is with you the whole evening.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Shanghai</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 12 · Plane trees, a commuter ferry, then south

*A low-rise morning to balance last night's skyline. Cross the river the way the city crosses it.*

**The former French Concession and Wukang Road**

plane trees over narrow streets, historic housing, small independent shops. Nothing here is tall, which after last night is the point.

**The Huangpu River ferry**

a few minutes across on the boat people take to work, standing at deck level with bicycles and commuters, the same water you looked at from above the night before.

**Flight to Shenzhen**

the afternoon goes to the airport and the flight, and nothing is scheduled on arrival. This is the second of the two light days.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Shenzhen</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 13 · Floor by floor

*Components, prototypes, finished machines, in that order and in that building. See what a city built for making actually looks like.*

**Huaqiangbei**

you climb through the market with your guide reading the trade as you go: trays of loose components on one floor, boards and prototypes above, finished machines above that, and the buyers moving between them with sample bags.

**What the guide adds**

not the products but the chain. Who sources from whom, at what volume, and why a shop selling one kind of connector can stay in business on one square meter. You will find yourself doing arithmetic on the way down the escalator.

**The rest of the day**

After visiting Huaqiangbei, you'll have free time to continue exploring the electronics market or return to your hotel to rest. If you'd like to add other Shenzhen experiences, these can be confirmed separately in a written quote.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Shenzhen</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 14 · Shenzhen to Guangzhou: Afternoon Tea Along the Pearl River

*On the final day, transfer from Shenzhen to Guangzhou for a Cantonese morning tea and some time to enjoy the views along both banks of the Pearl River.*

**High-Speed Rail from Shenzhen to Guangzhou**

On this day, take a high-speed train to Guangzhou, with the specific service coordinated with the return flight schedule. The order of morning tea and the train journey will be determined by the teahouse's location and the written itinerary.

**Haixinsha and Zhujiang New Town**

the contemporary waterfront of Guangzhou, towers and public space along the Pearl River. Fourteen days ago you were standing at a palace gate finished in 1420.

**Departure**

your flight home leaves from Guangzhou. The departure transfer is not part of the base price on this route, and whether your order carries it is written into your quote before you pay.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
- **Health & medications:** Bring a full supply of any prescription medication. International pharmacies exist in major cities but may not carry your brand. Travel insurance is required — we can recommend policies that cover China specifically.
- **Language:** English fluency outside hotels and tourist sites is limited. Your guides all speak English. We provide a printed pocket card with essential Chinese phrases and emergency contacts for every traveler.

## Before you book — What Each City Is For FAQ

### Six cities in fourteen days. Is this a bus tour with better hotels?

No, and the nights are how you check. Nine of the fourteen days run out of three hotels, three nights each in Beijing, Xi'an and Chengdu, so the first two thirds of the trip has no packing in it. Two days in the middle are deliberately slow, one of them an afternoon in a park teahouse with nothing after it. The moving happens on four rail legs and two flights, and each of those days carries a short morning rather than a full one.

### Why is the trip not open for booking?

Because our own review has not closed. In plain terms: the fourteen-day plan currently runs over on time once transfers are counted, the departure-day routing and the tea house's city are not fixed, the Shenzhen day needs a second confirmed item, Until those close we take no orders and no deposits. What you can do now is send us your dates: we will hold nothing, promise nothing and write to you the day it opens.

### Can we ride one of the driverless taxis in Shenzhen?

It can be arranged, as an optional add-on priced separately, and it is deliberately not part of the plan. The service runs on a commercial operator's own network, so its availability is not ours to promise: we book it against your date and reconfirm after you land, and if the operator is not running that day the ride does not happen and nothing is charged for it. That is exactly why it is not in your base price and not on the highlight list. What day thirteen gives you either way is Huaqiangbei, floor by floor, with a guide who reads the sourcing chain for you.

### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. Most European passports enter visa-free for stays days, and so do Canadian, Australian and New Zealand ones; the named list is in Good to Know. We check your fourteen days plus any other days you spend in China against that window before you pay. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, so departures on or before December 18, 2026 finish inside it. US passports are the exception and this route is not sold to US guests at present: the US is not on that list, fourteen days is longer than the ten-day transit policy allows, and our compliance review of that market is still open.

### How much walking is there really?

Every touring day is moderate walking on mostly flat ground, with two exceptions worth planning for. Jingshan on day two is stairs to the top, about an hour, and it is optional on the day if anyone would rather wait at the bottom. Mutianyu on day three is the real one: the cable car, chairlift and toboggan are all excluded from the base price, so without an add-on the climb to the wall is on foot. Tell us your party's walking range when you write, and we will tell you which of the two to add before you pay rather than after.
