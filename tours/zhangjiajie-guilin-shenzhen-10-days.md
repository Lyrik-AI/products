Ascend to Yuanjiajie via the Bailong Elevator to view the sandstone peak forest from a high vantage point.

**Yuanjiajie**

Walk from China's No. 1 Bridge to the Bewilderment Platform and gaze down at the sandstone pillars and valley below.

**Tianzi Mountain**

View the Imperial Brush Peak and Xihai Stone Forest from the Tianzi Mountain Viewing Platform; visibility of the distant peaks varies with weather.

### Day 3 · Golden Whip Stream & Huangshi Village

*In the morning, stroll along Jinbian Stream at the foot of the karst peaks; in the afternoon, take a cable car up Huangshi Village.*

**Golden Whip Stream**

a walk on the valley floor: clear water beside you, dense forest around you, and the sandstone walls going straight up on both sides; the length is cut to fit your pace.

**Looking up**

Looking up from the valley floor, sandstone cliffs rise on both sides of the stream—a perspective distinct from yesterday's view from Yuanjiajie.

**Huangshi Village**

the cable car lifts you to open platforms in the afternoon; below you, the pillar country runs to the horizon, and the geology finally reads as one connected landscape.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Wulingyuan</sub>

**The cableway**

Take the cable car up from Zhangjiajie city center and enjoy panoramic views of the urban landscape and mountain terrain from the cabin.

**Cliffside Walkway**

Walk along the cliffside trail on the mountain; visibility into the distant valley varies with the weather.

**Heavenly Gate Arch**

Stand at the foot of Tianmen Cave and look up through the natural opening piercing the mountain's body.

*South by rail to Guilin; an evening stroll along the Two Rivers and Four Lakes.*

**Rail south**

Take a train from Zhangjiajie West Station to Guilin, then head to your hotel upon arrival.

**Two Rivers & Four Lakes on foot**

an evening walk along the waterways that ring the city center: lit pagodas standing on the lakes, gardens along the banks, the pace set by however the train day left you.

*Cruise the Li River to Yangshuo, with the afternoon reserved for check-in and free time.*

Board a Li River cruise in the morning and drift downstream to Yangshuo.

**The river**

View the karst peaks, bamboo groves, and riverside villages from the boat; when the water is calm, you can even see the peaks reflected in the river.

**West Street evening**

you land in Yangshuo in the afternoon and check in; the evening is yours on West Street or along the riverfront of this busy little river town.

weather permitting, the day opens early at a panoramic viewpoint above the Li River, karst peaks stacked away to the horizon while the light comes up.

**Into the countryside**

rice fields, villages that still farm, quiet paths along the water; the day is built with stopping in mind: for photographs, for village life, for nothing in particular.

**Your speed**

Choose between cycling, walking, or a Yulong River bamboo raft based on your preference; whether the raft is available depends on the water level and safety regulations of the day.

**Rail south**

the high-speed train carries you from the karst country to Shenzhen; peaks outside your window at departure, towers by arrival.

**The new city on foot**

you walk the Civic Center district and Gangxia North Station, where contemporary design and urban planning are on open display, then follow the water at Shenzhen Bay.

**Shenzhen Bay after dark**

Just a few days ago, we were exploring the sandstone peak forests of Zhangjiajie. Tonight, we arrive at Shenzhen Bay, where the coastline is lined with urban architecture and dazzling night lights.

**Huaqiangbei**

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
- **Health & medications:** Bring a full supply of any prescription medication. International pharmacies exist in major cities but may not carry your brand. Travel insurance is required — we can recommend policies that cover China specifically.
