### Day 5 · Eight thousand men, for one man

*One site, one day, no filler. The scale is not the point here; the individual faces are.*

**The Terracotta Warriors**

an estimated eight thousand figures in formation, dug out where they stood and left there, so you are looking down into the excavation rather than at objects in cases. **The newer excavation areas**

traces of the original pigment are still visible on some figures here. They were painted, all of them, and the gray you have seen in photographs is what happened after the air got in.

### Day 9 · Someone divided a river

*A full day out of the city, and the reason this route exists. Today the trip stops being a list of places and becomes an argument about water.*

**Dujiangyan irrigation system**

begun in the 3rd century BC, and it splits the Min River in two without a dam. One arm is turned onto the plain to irrigate it; the other is left to take the flood away. You walk out over the divide with both arms of the river under you and your guide shows you the three parts doing the work: the head that splits the flow, the spillway that dumps the excess, and the cut through the rock that meters what goes through.

**Why it is not a ruin**

this is not a site that used to work. The Chengdu plain has been fed by it continuously since it was built, which is the difference between a monument and a machine.

**Mount Qingcheng**

Head to Mount Qingcheng to visit an active Taoist temple, chat with the resident priests, and practice Tai Chi in front of the temple. The chosen walking routes do not require the cable car, which is not included in the price.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

## Before you book — Two Kinds of Water FAQ

### The route is called Two Kinds of Water and you cannot sell me either of them. Why is this page up?

Because the honest answer is more useful to you than an empty page. Eleven of the 21 days are fully sourced and priced today: Beijing, Xi'an, Chengdu and Shanghai, with the water thread already running through Dujiangyan and Zhujiajiao. The two legs that give the route its name, the Jiuzhaigou valley and the Yangtze gorges, are designed and not bought: no operator, no valley-mouth hotel, no ship cabin grade, no carrier for the Yichang flight. Until those are named in writing we take no orders and no deposits, and nothing on days 10 to 18 is priced into any quote. If you tell us your dates we will write to you on the day it opens, and if you want the shorter fully-sourced version now, we will quote that instead.

### 21 days is a long time. Is that just the same trip stretched out? 21

No, and the difference shows up in the middles of days rather than in extra sights. Four cities get three nights each, so you unpack four times in three weeks instead of eight. Four days across the trip carry nothing scheduled at all, including a whole afternoon in a teahouse chair. Both rail transfers are half-days, not sprints. And the extra length is what makes the route's actual subject possible: the argument about water needs the 3rd-century BC waterworks, the dam and the courtyard pond in the same trip, which does not fit in fourteen days.

### Which dates can I actually go?

This season, a 12-day window: departures between October 8 and 19, 2026. Three rules stack up to produce it. The valley is unavailable November through March on our seasonal record, and on a 21-day plan the valley days fall 9 to 12 days after departure, so October 19 is the last departure whose valley days land in October. There are no departures October 1 to 7, the national holiday week. And once the river leg is sourced, the sailing on file leaves Chongqing on Fridays and boarding is day 15, so the departure day has to be a Friday too: October 9 and October 16. The October 16 date needs one confirmation from the operator first, because that boarding day is also listed for a special long-haul sailing. We check all of this against your dates before anything is charged.

### How high does this go, and what does the altitude day feel like?

You sleep at about 2,000 meters for four nights at the mouth of the valley, and the valley itself runs up to about 3,100 meters on the day inside it. That is high enough to notice and well below the range where the design would have to change. Two things in the plan are there for this: you climb to it by rail over about three and a half hours instead of flying, so your body gets the gain gradually, and the arrival day carries nothing at all. The valley day itself is walking on level boardwalks between shuttle stops rather than climbing. If anyone in your party has a condition affected by altitude, tell us before we talk dates.

### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. UK, most European and Canadian passports enter visa-free for stays days, and so do Australian and New Zealand passports. Before you pay we check your 21 days plus any extra days in China against that window. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, so departures on or before December 11, 2026 finish inside it. US passports are different: the US is not on that list, and 21 days is more than twice the stay the 10-day transit policy allows, so US guests hold a Chinese tourist or business visa, with the fee and the appointment their own. One more thing we say plainly: if you are already in China on the transit policy, you cannot take this route, because 21 days is far beyond it and the valley is outside the area that policy lets you stay in. We would rather say that than sell you something that does not work.
