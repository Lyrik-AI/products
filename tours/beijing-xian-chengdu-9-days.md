## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.

## Before you book — Two Capitals, Then Chengdu FAQ

### Nine days for Beijing, Xi'an and Chengdu. Is that too fast? 9

It is compact, and it is built to stay that way rather than to hide it. Three cities, three hotels, and both intercity moves are afternoon trains, so no day starts at an airport before dawn. Beijing gets three nights and Xi'an three, which is what the two of them need; Chengdu gets two, and one of those afternoons is a teahouse with nothing after it. The trade is real and we name it: there is no fourth city and no coastal stop on this plan.

### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. UK and European passports enter visa-free for stays days, and so do Canadian, Australian and New Zealand ones. We check your nine days plus any extra days in China against that window before you pay. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, so an entry on or before December 23, 2026 finishes inside it. US passports are different. The US is not on that list, so US guests hold a Chinese tourist or business visa. The fee and the appointment are their own, and we confirm visa status before any payment. This route is not sold on the ten-day visa-free transit policy this season.

### Can we fly straight home from Chengdu?

From the UK and western Europe, yes: Chengdu has direct long-haul service to your markets. From the Nordic countries, central Europe, Ireland and North America it is thin, so most guests connect. We do not include international tickets, so where there is no direct service the connection flies on your own ticket and your airline arranges it. If you would rather we add a domestic leg out of Chengdu to reach another gateway, we price it separately when you choose it and check the connecting time against your own flight. One thing to settle early either way: day nine tours through the afternoon, so tell us your departure time before you book.

### A whole afternoon in a teahouse. Isn't that half a day wasted?

It is the one afternoon we will argue for. Chengdu's teahouses are where the city actually spends its time, and an hour there reads as a stop on the way somewhere; three hours reads as the thing itself. Your tea and the refills are included, your guide stays or steps away as you prefer, and the park sits twenty minutes' walk from the shopping streets if part of your group wants to go and come back. If you would still rather be moving, say so when we talk: we can put the opera on that afternoon instead and leave day nine lighter before your flight.

### Why aren't lunches and dinners included?

On purpose, and it saves you money on this particular route. Two of your nine days are built around eating on the street, the Muslim Quarter in Xi'an and the lanes in Chengdu, where a set menu paid in advance would be the worst way to eat. Your guide recommends and books each day around what you feel like, and you pay at the table. All eight hotel breakfasts are included.
