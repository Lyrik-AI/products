## Overview

For most first-time visitors, a China trip begins in Beijing, with a Palace Museum tour on day two. This journey instead starts in the south, opening in a city busy with modernization, then heading northward without retracing its steps: a temple courtyard in Foshan, the Shanghai skyline with its two banks gazing across the river, an unstructured afternoon in a Chengdu teahouse, an irrigation system in operation since the third century BCE, followed by the first emperor's army, then the palace, and finally the Great Wall. Three nights each in Chengdu, Xi'an, and Beijing let the second half of the itinerary slow down. By day thirteen, when the Great Wall finally appears, you'll realize you've long since understood exactly what you're looking at.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.

## Before you book — The Wall Comes Last FAQ

### Why does the Great Wall come on day thirteen?

Because it is the last piece, not the first. Put it on day two and it stays the picture you already had before you booked. Put it on day thirteen and you reach it having stood in a Foshan temple courtyard, on the Bund between two skylines, at an irrigation system from the 3rd century BC that is still running, and in front of the first emperor's army, so the wall arrives as an answer rather than a photograph. The practical side works out the same way: the round trip from the city takes most of a day, so the wall gets a day of its own with nothing heavy beside it.

### Fourteen days, five cities, five intercity movements. How much of that is travel?

Less than the numbers suggest, because none of it doubles back. The two flights sit in the afternoon of days three and five, each with a morning of sightseeing before it. Two of the three rail movements are afternoon departures on days eight and eleven, again after a morning out. The third is a same-day round trip on day seven that leaves your Chengdu room untouched. Your own driver runs both ends of every movement and your guide hands you over at the door. What that buys you is the second half: three nights each in Chengdu, Xi'an and Beijing, one hotel per city.

### We're traveling in November. What actually changes?

Three things on this route move with the weather. The Mutianyu ascent runs on a cableway and an open chairlift, and snow or ice closes both. The wall-top cycling section in Xi'an is the longest unsheltered stretch on the route. The hill paths at Mount Qingcheng are the third. We cannot promise any of the three against a forecast, and we will not pretend otherwise: your quote states in writing what is arranged for your exact dates. What holds regardless: the wall day is yours either way and the section is walked when it is not ridden, so you stand on the same wall and in the same place on the ramparts, and the two indoor anchors of the trip, the palace and the army, are unaffected by weather.

### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. British, French, German, Dutch, Belgian, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Greek, Nordic, Central European, Irish, Canadian, Australian and New Zealand passports currently enter visa-free for stays days, and we check your 14 days plus any extra days in China against that window before you pay. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, so departures on or before December 18, 2026 finish inside it. US passports are different: the US is not on that list, and 14 days is longer than the 10-day visa-free transit policy allows, so US guests hold a Chinese tourist or business visa, applied for in person the first time. We confirm visa status before any payment, and if the dates do not fit, we say so instead of selling.
