## Overview

Everyone photographs the Bund, but few ask why this city was built where it stands. The answer is in the water: the ponds within the Ming-dynasty garden walls, the commuter ferries crossing the Huangpu River, and the canals that serve as streets in Zhujiajiao. Spend the first four nights in Shanghai with a day trip to a water town; then head to Suzhou for one night, followed by a night in Hangzhou. Your seventh day ends in Hangzhou, and the gardens and waterways along the way may change how you see the water in your own city.

*Water inside a wall, then water as a border. Two versions of the same city, six hours apart.*

you start inside the Ming garden, following covered walks between rockery, pond and pavilions, past the pitted limestone rock the garden is known for. **Shanghai Museum**

your reserved slot is booked before you land, so you walk in rather than queue for one. Bronzes, ceramics, painting and calligraphy, and the decorative arts: your guide takes one gallery properly rather than all of them at speed.

**The Bund at dusk**

you walk the embankment as the lights come on, stone banks at your back, towers across the water, and your guide beside you the whole way. Somewhere along the railing you will stop mid-sentence, because both sides of the river are asking you the same question.

### Day 3 · Plane trees, the ferry, the deck

*The city at street level, at water level, and from the top. Find out which one tells you the most.*

**The former French Concession and Wukang Road on foot**

you walk under plane trees along Wukang Road and the streets off it, past 1920s and 1930s villas and apartment buildings that people still live in, with small shops on the ground floors. Your guide tells you who built what and who lives there now.

you queue with the commuters, walk on with them, and stand at the rail while barges and container tugs pass. This is the working river, and the crossing costs about what a bus ride costs. By the middle of the river you will notice that you have not checked your phone once.

the lift takes you up on the Pudong side, and the delta opens out below you: the bend of the river, the far bank you walked last night, the old city and its garden as a patch of dark roofs, and the creeks running west toward the water towns you meet tomorrow.

### Day 4 · Zhujiajiao

*Take a punt boat through the water towns, then return to Shanghai to rest in the evening.*

**Down the canal by hand-rowed boat**

an hour west of the city, a boatman rows you from the stern with a single oar. Doorsteps go straight down into the water; a woman rinses vegetables on the bottom step; the lanes climb to meet each bridge you pass under.

**Fangsheng Bridge and the stone lanes**

you come up onto the town's high stone arch and look down the canal in both directions, then walk the lanes on foot, which in this town means walking along the water, because there is no other way for a street to go.

**Küzhi Garden**

a walled garden house built by a family with the money to make a private landscape in a town where water was already everywhere: the same instinct you met inside Yu Garden two days ago, at a quarter of the scale.

**Back to Shanghai by lunchtime**

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.

## Before you book — Shanghai, by Water FAQ

### Seven days are listed, but only four are priced. What is going on?

Four days are settled and three are not. The Shanghai and Zhujiajiao days have contracted suppliers, named admissions and a price. The Suzhou and Hangzhou days are designed but the ground handling there is not yet placed, so we will not price them and will not describe experiences we cannot yet deliver. You lose nothing by asking: we tell you in writing which version you are being quoted, and the four-day plan stands complete on its own.

### Do I need a visa?

Most of our guests do not. These passports currently enter China visa-free for up to 30 days: British · French · German · Dutch · Belgian · Italian · Spanish · Portuguese · Greek · Nordic · Central European · Irish · Canadian · Australian · New Zealand. US passports do need a Chinese tourist or business visa for this route, and the fee and appointment are your own; a 10-year multiple-entry visa works. We check your entry permission against your dates before you pay anything.

### A commuter ferry instead of a river cruise? Really?

Yes, and it is deliberate. The cruise shows you the skyline you already saw from the Bund, from further away, with a commentary. The ferry costs about a bus fare, runs all day, and puts you on an open deck among people going to work, with the barges and container tugs that are the whole reason this city exists passing close by. If you also want the cruise, we will book it separately and tell you the fare.

### Can you actually get us into the Shanghai Museum?

We book the timed slot for you rather than leaving it to you, because the museum's own reservation system carries limited English support. We cannot guarantee any single date: slots are released on the museum's own schedule and popular days go. If your day two cannot be booked, we tell you before you pay and name the replacement in writing, and the two Shanghai touring days can swap over so the museum runs on day three instead.

### Why are lunches and dinners not included?

On purpose. Shanghai is the wrong city in which to prepay set menus: the range runs from a bowl of noodles under a plane tree to a dining room with a view of the river, and tastes diverge by the second day. Breakfast is included at the hotel each morning; for the rest, your guide recommends and books around what you actually feel like eating, and you pay for it there.
