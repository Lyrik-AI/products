## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1 · Shanghai: Old Town Gardens and Riverside Night Views

*After meeting your Chinese-speaking driver-guide, walk from Shanghai's old town all the way to the Huangpu River. Stops and meals for the day are arranged at your own pace by the same group of guests.*

**Yu Garden and City God Temple Area**

Begin at the halls, covered bridges, and rockeries of Yu Garden, then stroll into the adjacent old-town lanes. The garden's intimate scale meets the flow of the commercial district, allowing you to experience both traditional landscaping and contemporary urban life in a single walk.

**Nanjing Road Pedestrian Street**

Walk along Nanjing Road toward the Huangpu River, passing the streetscape of Shanghai's central business district and its time-honored shops. This is a clear transition from the old city into modern Shanghai.

**Huangpu River Night Cruise**

After nightfall, board a cruise at Shiliupu Wharf to see the historic buildings of the Bund and the Pudong skyline in the same view. As the boat glides along the river, the illuminated cityscape on both banks unfolds section by section.

<sub>• **Accommodation:** Shanghai</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Lunch and dinner are at your own expense; for dinner, you may refer to Shenpin Hui. Recommendations do not imply reservations have been made.</sub>

### Day 2 · Shanghai and Zhouzhuang: From Urban Memories to an Ancient Town Night Tour

*Spend the morning exploring Shanghai's modern and contemporary neighborhoods, then take our private car to Zhouzhuang in the afternoon. Dinner and evening sightseeing can be arranged to match the pace of your fellow travelers.*

**Sihang Warehouse**

Visit the Sihang Warehouse, a site tied to the 1937 Battle of Shanghai, and learn about this chapter of Shanghai's modern history through the building itself. The preserved traces of the structure ensure that history lives on beyond the pages of books.

**Wukang Road & Columbia Circle**

Walk past the modern and contemporary buildings on Wukang Road, then enter Columbia Circle, a complex renovated from historic structures. Placing the two together offers a contrast that shows how Shanghai's neighborhoods have evolved from residential spaces of the past to the present day.

**Zhouzhuang Boat Ride & Night Tour**

After lunch, take a private car to Zhouzhuang, and first enjoy the included boat ride across the ancient town from the river. After choosing your own dinner, walk back into the alleys and continue the night tour along the stone bridges and waterside residences.

<sub>• **Accommodation:** Near Zhouzhuang Ancient Town</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Hotel breakfast; lunch and dinner on your own, with recommendations such as Kong Yiji Hotel and Mulan Suzhou Cuisine.</sub>

### Day 3 · Suzhou and Wuzhen: Temples, Gardens, Pingtan, and Water Alleys

*Spend a day weaving through Suzhou's temples, gardens, and opera, then take a private car to stay in Xizha, Wuzhen. Meals are at your own choice, not limited to fixed group dining.*

**Hanshan Temple and Lingering Garden**

Start at Hanshan Temple beside the ancient canal west of Suzhou, then pass through the halls, corridors, framed views, and rockery spaces of the Lingering Garden. From temple to garden, two distinct Jiangnan spaces unfold in a single morning.

**Seven-mile Shantang Canal Cruise with Suzhou Pingtan**

From the water of Shantang River, view the riverside houses and bridges, then listen to a live performance of Suzhou Pingtan. The strings, singing, and Wu dialect transform the Suzhou you saw by day into an audible memory of the city.

**Night Tour of Xizha, Wuzhen**

In the afternoon, take a private car to Wuzhen and check in at the Xizha Scenic Area. After choosing your own dinner, take a stroll along the waterways and bridges at night, then return directly to your accommodation within the scenic area.

<sub>• **Accommodation:** Inside Xizha Scenic Area, Wuzhen</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Hotel breakfast; lunch and dinner on your own, with recommendations for Xiangji Su-style Cuisine and Tong'an Inn Chinese Restaurant.</sub>

### Day 4 · Wuzhen and Hangzhou: From Morning Waterways to Xixi Wetland

*Start the day by retracing the morning paths of Xizha, staying within the scenic area, then take a private car into the wetland waterways of Hangzhou. Wrap up the evening with a grand stage performance.*

**Morning visit to Xizha again**

Walk along the water alleys of Xizha in the early morning, passing once more the bridges, docks, and waterfront houses seen last night. Staying within the scenic area saves you the hassle of re-entering, allowing you to witness a different rhythm of the ancient town before the crowds arrive.

**Xixi Wetland Waterway**

After a self-selected lunch, proceed to Hangzhou, where you'll take a boat tour through part of Xixi Wetland and use the scenic area's electric shuttle to connect between different zones. Arrive in Hangzhou via narrow waterways, reeds, and riverside villages, rather than just along city roads.

**Songcheng Eternal Love**

In the evening, enjoy a VIP seat performance of Songcheng Eternal Love. The show uses a grand stage, dance, and scene changes to weave together stories related to Hangzhou. The exact showtime is subject to final confirmation.

<sub>• **Accommodation:** Hangzhou, first night of a two-night stay</sub>

### Day 5 · Hangzhou: Temples, Tea Villages, and the Waters of West Lake

*Walk from the Feilai Peak stone carvings to Meijiawu Tea Village, then look back at the mountains and causeways from the surface of West Lake. Lunch is at your own choice and expense.*

**Feilai Peak and Lingyin Temple**

Start with the Feilai Feng stone carvings, then enter the Lingyin Temple grounds. The itinerary includes internal shuttle service, with the walking focus on the relationship between the carvings, the temple spaces, and the mountain forest environment.

**Meijiawu Tea Tasting**

At noon, head to Meijiawu to taste local tea in the tea village and choose your own lunch. Transition from the city's temples to tea fields and villages, and learn how Hangzhou tea becomes part of daily life.

**West Lake Boat Ride, Su Causeway, and Flower Harbor Fish Viewing**

Cruise across part of West Lake, identifying the embankments, islets, and distant hills from the water. After disembarking, continue on foot along Su Causeway and view fish at Flower Harbor Park, connecting the distant views from the boat with the close-up scenery by the lakeshore.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
