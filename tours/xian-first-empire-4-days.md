### 01. Terra Cotta Warriors

<sub>Xi'an · Heritage</sub>

*the excavation halls outside Xi'an holding the underground army of Qin Shi*

<p>

In the excavation halls on the outskirts of Xi'an lies the underground army of China’s first emperor, Qin Shi Huang, and one of the country’s most significant archaeological discoveries. Look down into the pits where the soldiers stand in formation: each face is unique, the armor varies by rank, and even after more than two millennia buried, the ranks remain clearly distinct. Your guide will help you identify which figures are officers, which are archers, and what these formations were originally designed for. Why it’s worth a slow visit: the sheer scale is immediately striking, but the craftsmanship details reward you when you get close.

### 05. Giant Wild Goose Pagoda

<sub>Xi'an · Pagoda</sub>

*The Tang pagoda in the Great Ci'en Temple, built under the supervision of Xuanzang to house the sutras he brought back from India.*

<p>
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/2be95bfc5748.jpg" alt="Giant Wild Goose Pagoda" width="32%">
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/eb70bbcc8abb.jpg" alt="Giant Wild Goose Pagoda" width="32%">
<img src="https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/kfpEh4mT.webp" alt="Giant Wild Goose Pagoda" width="32%">
</p>

The Giant Wild Goose Pagoda stands within the Grand Compassion Temple (Daci'en Temple) on Ci'en Road in Xi'an's Yanta District. In 652 (the third year of Yonghui in the Tang Dynasty), Xuanzang led the construction of the pagoda to house the scriptures and Buddhist statues he brought back from India via the Silk Road. After subsequent renovations, it became Tang Chang'an's definitive landmark for Buddhism and sutra translation. When visiting, you can explore the temple courtyard and pagoda structure; tickets to climb the pagoda are sold on-site according to the day's designated hours. We recommend allowing 1.5–3 hours; during peak season, the site is typically open from 8:00 AM to 5:30 PM, with shorter hours in the off-season and entry closing around 5:00 PM, subject to the official daily schedule. The pagoda connects Tang-era Chang'an and the Silk Road history of Buddhist translation in a single brick structure, offering far more than just the plaza's fountains.

> A classic Tang-era landmark connected to ancient Chang'an history.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.

## Before you book — Xi'an, Layer by Layer FAQ

### Four days in one city. Is there enough in Xi'an?

Four days is what it takes to stop treating Xi'an as one museum stop. The warriors get a whole morning and their own round trip. The wall gets an afternoon on top of it. The last morning goes to a pagoda courtyard and an hour of pulse and herbs. In between, two half-days are left open on purpose, and you sleep in the same room for three nights with no stations in your week.

### Everyone says the warriors are packed. How bad is it?

It is a busy site and we do not pretend otherwise: it is one of the most visited sites in Shaanxi and the pits are indoor halls with fixed viewing rails. What we do is give it the morning rather than the middle of the day, keep nothing else on the schedule beside it, and let your guide work the pits in the order that thins out first. What we cannot promise is a quiet hall on a peak date. October 1 to 7 is the national holiday week, and for those dates your quote states the crowd situation in writing before you decide. Even on a full day, the rail is long and the ranks are readable: you will get your faces.

### I haven't ridden a bike in years. Is the wall ride hard?

No. The wall top is flat, paved and wide enough to ride two abreast, with no traffic on it. You set the pace and stop where you like. If you would rather not ride at all, or the day is wet, you walk a stretch of the same wall top instead and the price is identical. Nobody has to get on a bicycle to get the view.

### The Chinese medicine hour, is that a sales pitch?

No, and it is written into the plan that way. The hour is an introduction: your pulse is read, the drawers of the herb cabinet are opened and named, and you get the seasonal logic behind a prescription. That session is in your price. A physical treatment is offered afterwards, which you take or leave on the spot and pay the venue for only if you take it. No product purchase is part of the visit, and taking part never requires a treatment.

### How do I actually get to Xi'an, and do I need a visa?

Almost everyone connects. Xi'an has few nonstop long-haul flights, so most guests fly into Beijing or Shanghai and take a domestic leg on their own international ticket. Send us the connection you are holding and we build the four days around the hour you land. On visas: every market this route is sold to enters China visa-free for days, and the full list sits under Good to Know. We confirm your whole stay in China fits that window before you pay.
