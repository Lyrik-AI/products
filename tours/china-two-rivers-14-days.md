The pagoda connects Xi’an’s Tang Chang'an history with the Silk Road’s legacy of sutra translation in a single brick structure, rather than just being a backdrop for the square's fountains.

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
- **Health & medications:** Bring a full supply of any prescription medication. International pharmacies exist in major cities but may not carry your brand. Travel insurance is required — we can recommend policies that cover China specifically.

## Before you book — Two Rivers, Two Thousand Years FAQ

### Fourteen days, five cities. Is that too much moving for a first trip?

Four of the moves are high-speed rail and one is a flight, and each of them takes a half day rather than a whole one. You unpack five times in fourteen days, and both of the out-of-town days, Dujiangyan and Foshan, are day returns that leave your room and your bags where they are. Two days are deliberately easy: the Chengdu teahouse afternoon on day 2 and the Shanghai morning on day 12. If it still sounds like a lot, tell us and we cut a stop rather than speed the rest up.

### Can you actually get us into the Forbidden City?

Here is the honest state of it. Three places on this route, the Forbidden City, the National Museum of China and the Shanghai Museum, take a timed reservation registered to a name, and those systems are hard to use on an overseas phone number. Admission to all three is inside your price. What is not settled is who books the slots and at what cost, which is exactly why this route is not open for booking yet: we are not going to sell you a day that depends on a booking we cannot yet promise. Your quote will state in writing how each slot is handled, and if a date cannot be secured we rearrange that day and tell you before you pay. What holds either way: the Beijing days are never set on a Monday, when the palace closes.

### We're coming in December. Is that a bad time?

December is a fine month to see most of this route: the Terracotta Warriors and the museums are indoors, and the crowds are down everywhere. Two things are outdoors and we cannot promise them: the Mutianyu cable car and open chairlift on day 9 and the Xi'an City Wall ride on day 6. Their winter operating rules are being confirmed with the sites, so a December quote states in writing what happens to each one if the site suspends it on your day. If the wall ride is off, that morning goes to the wall on foot and the Bell and Drum Tower area instead; if the chairlift is off, the site's permitted alternative way up and down is used. Either way you get the wall and you get the wall day to yourself.

### None of us has done martial arts. Is the Wing Chun session going to be awkward?

It is built for people who have never done it. The practitioner opens with where the style comes from and what it is built on, so the first stretch is listening rather than doing. Then it is one stance and a few basic movements, with him walking around correcting your feet and elbows. There is no sparring, no fitness requirement and nothing to bring. It happens in the courtyards of Foshan Ancestral Temple, where the people performing the lion dance that morning actually train, which is the reason it is on this route rather than a hotel function room.

### Do we need a visa?

Most guests on this route do not. UK, most European, Canadian, Australian and New Zealand passports enter visa-free for stays days, and before you pay we check your 14 days plus any further days in China against that window. The current policy runs through December 31, 2026, so departures on or before December 18, 2026 finish inside it. US passports are different: the US is not on that list and 14 days is longer than the 10-day transit policy allows, so US guests hold a Chinese tourist or business visa and the fee and appointment are their own. We confirm visa status before any payment, and if your dates do not fit the window we say so instead of selling.
