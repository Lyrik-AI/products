### Day 6 · The other answer

*Out to the river in the morning and up the mountain after it, back in Chengdu by night. Today the country stops being a set of monuments and becomes a set of decisions.*

**High-speed rail out**

A short high-speed rail journey departing from Chengdu, with drivers meeting you at both ends. Enjoy four consecutive nights in the same room in Chengdu, and the hotel keeps it just as you left it.

**Dujiangyan Irrigation System**

begun in the 3rd century BC and still running. Your guide walks you along it and names the parts: this piece divides the flow, that one takes the excess away, the third one meters what goes through. Stand where the river comes apart and watch which way the water goes, then look at how old the idea is.

**What it is not**

Three days ago, you stood before a wall, built to stop something along a line. The Dujiangyan irrigation system, born of the same civilization, did the opposite: it didn't block the river; it divided it, letting it keep flowing. Both structures still stand today. Only Dujiangyan still performs its original function.

**Mount Qingcheng**

one of China's important Taoist mountains, half an hour away and part of the same day. You walk up through the trees to a quiet temple rather than the busiest one, and a Taoist who lives on the mountain sits down with you; your guide translates. Then a tai chi session, outdoors, on the mountain. The walking is on paths and steps, and the pace is set by your party rather than by a clock.

**Back to Chengdu**

In the evening, take the high-speed rail back to Chengdu, returning to the same room. You won't need to unpack or pack at either end of the day.

from the seats it reads as a trick. From two meters away it reads as a job, done fast, by hands that have done it several thousand times.

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### What if my inbound flight is delayed and eats hours off the window?

The window starts at your entry stamp, so a delay moves the whole ten days rather than shortening the last day only. That is why the plan is built with roughly a full day of margin inside the window, and why we compute your hours from your actual arrival time rather than from a schedule. What we cannot promise is a delay of any size: airlines and immigration halls are not ours. If a long delay compresses your window, the day-ten content is cut from the Shenzhen end and the crossing time stays where it is, because the crossing is the one thing that cannot move. What you still get in that case is the same guide, the same vehicle and an exit with its four-hour buffer intact.

It is the point of this route rather than a gap in it. Those four nights are one room held straight through, and they carry four different days: the panda base in the morning and a long afternoon in a teahouse, a rail day out to a 3rd-century-BC waterworks and a Taoist mountain, a day in the old lanes ending in a community opera theater and its backstage, and a quiet monastery morning before the flight south. Only two of the ten days involve moving hotels, which is the lightest transfer load we run on this window. ### Can we ride in a driverless taxi in Shenzhen?

These vehicles run on a commercial operator's routes and the operator decides day by day what is running, so the ride is not in your price and it is not part of what this plan sells you.
