## Overview

Two rivers, two bases, one move. You stay two nights in Guilin and two in Yangshuo; the third day's transit is the boat itself—four and a half hours downstream, which is also the day’s sightseeing. Guilin gets a full day, not just a stopover. The Yangshuo days are smaller and sharper: bamboo rafts on a tributary, cycling along field paths, an afternoon by the water, then nothing to do. On the final morning, before dawn, you step up onto the river bend where you drifted past. When you get home, you’ll know how it feels to do only one thing a day.

## Day-by-day itinerary

*No matter when you arrive, begin your first evening with the Lijiang and Four Lakes night cruise before heading to your hotel in Guilin. Guilin starts with the water, then the streets.*

Once your itinerary is confirmed, your driver will meet you at the arrival listed on the confirmation form—whether you’re flying in or arriving by high-speed train from a hub city. The drive into the city is short.

**Two Rivers and Four Lakes night cruise**

the boat takes the ring of water around the city center, two rivers joined to four lakes. Stand at the rail as the twin pagodas come up on the lake, one gold and one silver in the water, with banyan branches closing over the channel behind you. Your guide sets the sailing time against your actual arrival, so a late flight moves the boat rather than the day.

**Evening**

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** None included today</sub>

### Day 2 · The rock, above ground and below

*A cave in the morning, an old street in the afternoon. By evening you can read the landscape you came for.*

**Reed Flute Cave**

you walk a made path down into a limestone cave, past columns and curtains of stone that took the water a very long time to build, with the formations lit as you go. Morning is set for it because the cave is cool and the street is better later in the day.

**Guilin old town on foot**

pedestrian streets, then the snack lanes along the river. Your guide names what is in the pans before you decide whether to eat it, and anything you try is on your own account. The city is named for the osmanthus tree, and in autumn you smell it before you see it.

**Pace**

this walk stretches or shortens to fit the afternoon, and there is nothing scheduled after it.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Guilin</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 3 · One river, all day

*The move to Yangshuo is the boat, so no day is spent getting anywhere. Today is the day the trip stops being a list.*

**Board at the Guilin pier**

Your car will take you to the Guilang dock, and once you disembark in Yangshuo, you’ll be picked up for your hotel. The cabin class for this booking is specified in the quotation; any class not listed should not be assumed confirmed.

**Li River full cruise**

about four and a half hours with the current. The peaks come past in rows, the near ones sharp and the far ones going pale, and the river keeps folding around them so the view resets every few minutes. Take the deck rather than the window seat for the first hour. Somewhere in the second hour you'll notice you have stopped asking how much further it is.

**Lunch on board**

The ship’s crossing spans midday. Whether the on-board lunch is included in the base fare will be stated in the quote; if not, it is not considered included.

**Arrival in Yangshuo**

the boat ties up and your vehicle takes you to the hotel you will not leave for two nights.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Yangshuo</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 4 · A small river after a big one

*Yesterday was one long look at the water. Today you get down onto it and then sit beside it.*

**Yulong River countryside cycling**

you ride out through paddies and farmhouses on tracks a car cannot take, with the peaks standing up out of the flat fields on both sides. Bicycle, electric bike or riding in the vehicle alongside: decided on the morning by whoever in your party wants the least effort. The price is the same either way.

**Yulong River bamboo raft**

an afternoon on a selected section of the small river, the deck sitting low enough that the water runs a hand's width beneath your feet, and low weirs stepping the raft down with a slap and a splash. Trail one hand over the side and film the front of the raft against the hills.

**Riverside afternoon tea**

one serving each, mid-afternoon, at a table by the water with the hills on three sides and again in the water in front of you. Nothing is scheduled after it. By the second cup you'll notice that nobody in your party has looked at a phone since the raft.

**No evening item**

We’ve intentionally left tonight open. We’ll set off before dawn tomorrow.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Yangshuo</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

### Day 5 · Above the bend

*Up before light, over the river you came down. The last morning gives the four days behind it their shape.*

**Xianggong Hill sunrise**

the drive out goes in the dark and the stone steps take about twenty minutes with the town still asleep behind you. At the platform the river lies below in a long bend, and as the light arrives the peaks turn silver, then gold, the far rows last.

**Xingping Ancient Town**

stone streets and shophouses after sunrise, with the town opening its shutters around you. The river beside it is the stretch printed on the back of China's 20-yuan banknote, so hold up a note and line it up if you are carrying one. Walking out of the last lane you'll feel the five days close into a single picture.

**Drive back to Guilin**

your vehicle runs you from Yangshuo back to Guilin airport or the railway station, timed against your onward departure with room to spare rather than trimmed to the minute.

<sub>🛏️ **Stay:** Trip ends</sub>

<sub>🍽️ **Meals:** Breakfast at the hotel</sub>

## China Ready

We've solved the hard parts. Here's everything a first-time Western traveler needs to know before arriving.

### 🔐 VPN & Internet Access

<sub>Do this before you fly</sub>

- **What's blocked:** Google, Gmail, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and most Western news sites are inaccessible in China without a VPN.
- **Download before you leave:** It's nearly impossible to download a VPN once inside China. Install and test at home. Recommended: Astrill (most reliable), Mullvad, ExpressVPN — subscribe to at least two in case one stops working.
- **Hotel Wi-Fi tip:** Some hotels block VPN connections on their Wi-Fi. Use your phone's mobile data as a hotspot if the VPN drops.
- **Good news:** WhatsApp works perfectly through a VPN, so your messaging stays unchanged. Apple iMessage and Signal also work.

### 💳 Paying in China

<sub>Your concierge sets this up on Day 1</sub>

- **China is cashless:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used for almost everything — restaurants, taxis, markets, tickets. Cash is rarely accepted in cities.
- **International cards now work:** Foreign Visa/Mastercard can now link directly to Alipay (set up in minutes, \$5,000 single transaction limit). Your concierge walks you through the setup on your first morning.
- **Carry some cash:** We recommend ¥500–800 RMB in cash for the rare vendor that won't accept digital payment, and for small towns.
- **Booking this trip:** Your deposit and balance are charged via Stripe — the same trusted platform used by Airbnb and Booking.com. USD billing, no surprise conversion fees.

### 🛂 Visa & Entry

<sub>We handle the paperwork</sub>

- **Do you need a visa?:** Most US, UK, EU, Australian, Canadian, and New Zealand passport holders need a tourist (L) visa for stays over 10 days. We provide a visa invitation letter with every booking — it significantly speeds up approval.
- **240-hour visa-free transit:** Citizens of 55+ countries (including USA, UK, most of EU, AUS, NZ, Canada) can enter China visa-free for up to 10 days if transiting through eligible ports. This covers our 12-day itinerary only if you have a confirmed onward flight.
- **Processing time:** Apply 4–6 weeks before departure. Standard processing: 4–7 business days. Express (3 days) and same-day options available at most Chinese consulates.
- **What we provide:** Visa invitation letter, hotel confirmation for all nights, suggested itinerary document — everything the consulate needs, formatted correctly.

### 🛡️ Safety & Practical Tips

<sub>Our responsibility, always</sub>

- **Is China safe?:** China consistently ranks among the safest countries for foreign tourists. Violent crime against visitors is extremely rare. Cities have extensive CCTV coverage and a visible police presence.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: 110. Ambulance: 120. Your concierge is your first call for any non-emergency situation — she has local contacts for every city on the itinerary.
- **Health & medications:** Bring a full supply of any prescription medication. International pharmacies exist in major cities but may not carry your brand. Travel insurance is required — we can recommend policies that cover China specifically.
- **Language:** English fluency outside hotels and tourist sites is limited. Your guides all speak English. We provide a printed pocket card with essential Chinese phrases and emergency contacts for every traveler.

## Before you book — Downstream, Then Up FAQ

### There are no direct flights to Guilin. How do we actually get there?

Through one hub, and we plan it with you before anything is charged. From Europe, the UK, North America or Australasia you fly into Hong Kong, Shenzhen, Guangzhou, Beijing or Shanghai, then take a short flight or a high-speed train on to Guilin. From Shenzhen the train runs about three hours. That leg is not inside this plan's price, and your quote states in writing which hub you use, who books that leg and who pays for it. We also check your day-one arrival time and your day-five departure time against the plan before we take the order.

### Four and a half hours on a boat. Is that too long?

It is the point of the day rather than the cost of it. That sailing is also how you get from Guilin to Yangshuo, so it replaces a road transfer instead of adding to one, and nothing else is scheduled around it. You are on deck with the peaks passing on both banks, there are seats inside when you want them, and the boat class booked for your order is written into your quote. If sitting still for an afternoon is not how you want to spend a fifth of your trip, this is the route to think twice about.

### We're coming in November or December. Will the river be too low?

The Li River is a river, and its late-autumn and winter level is set by rainfall, not by us. We are confirming this selling window's conditions with our operator now, and no one can guarantee a river's depth months ahead. What we do instead: for any departure from November 1, 2026 your quote states in writing what happens to the day-three sailing where the operator shortens or reroutes it, and we will not take your order without that in writing. Whatever happens to the sailing, the two nights in Yangshuo, the Yulong River day and the Xianggong Hill sunrise are unaffected. They sit on a different river and on land.

### Getting up before dawn on our last day, with a flight to catch. Does that work?

It works, and it is checked against your actual departure time before we take your order. The hill is about two and a half hours including the drive out and the climb, and you are back down for breakfast time. Xingping Ancient Town follows, then the drive from Yangshuo to Guilin airport or the railway station, timed with room to spare. That is also why day four carries no evening item: the evening before a pre-dawn start belongs to sleeping. Where your departure is early enough that the sequence does not fit, we say so before you book rather than after.

Not yet, and we would rather say so than take your money. the remaining items have to be settled first. the boat class on the day-three sailing; who books and pays for your connection into Guilin; and this route's remaining pre-listing review.
