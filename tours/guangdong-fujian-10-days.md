## Things to do

### 01. Shape your own red peach cakes

<sub>Teochew · Handmade</sub>

*A shop on Paifang Street, with red-yeast dough, a carved mould and a steamer*

![Shape your own red peach cakes](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/93f2e60215bb.webp)

Hong Tao Jiao, a traditional Chaoshan glutinous rice cake shaped like a peach for auspiciousness, is a staple offering for ancestor worship and holiday celebrations. Under the guidance of the shop’s craftsman, you’ll knead dough infused with red yeast rice, fill it with a savory glutinous rice mixture, press the dough into a carved peach-shaped wooden mold, and then steam it until cooked. The entire process takes about an hour, and you can take your freshly steamed cakes home with you. The shop is located beneath the arcade of Paifang Street, making it perfect to continue your shopping or strolling immediately after.

> Included in the tour; no separate booking is needed. Larger groups are split into two sittings.

### 02. The flower-adorned hair of Xunpu Village

<sub>Quanzhou · Folk Traditions</sub>

*A ring of fresh flowers coiled at the back of the head, held by an ivory pin*

![The flower-adorned hair of Xunpu Village](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/b6e4994cf210.webp)

Wander the narrow lanes of Xunpu Village to admire the exterior walls of oyster-shell houses, then enjoy a zanhua makeup experience during your reserved time slot. With fresh flowers pinned at the back of your head, you can arrange photo shoots on the lanes or in indoor settings; specific scenes and timing will be confirmed on the day.

> Included in the tour. Styling and photography run to the day's booked slot; in rain the session moves to the verandas and indoor settings rather than being cancelled.

### 03. Host a tea tasting at an old tea factory

<sub>Quanzhou · Tea</sub>

*The Anxi State-Owned Tea Factory, in the home of Tieguanyin*

![Host a tea tasting at an old tea factory](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/a2756c7fbe7f.webp)

Anxi is the birthplace of Tie Guan Yin tea. At the processing factory, observe the withering, rolling, and roasting stages, then join a cupping session—comparing the aroma, liquor color, and wet leaves of three identical cups. If your visit coincides with the tea harvest, explore picking in the gardens the next day; otherwise, continue observing the production process and participate in cupping. Specific itineraries depend on the season.

> Included in the tour. Picking in the fields follows the tea season, mainly spring and autumn; outside that season the factory process and the tasting remain.
