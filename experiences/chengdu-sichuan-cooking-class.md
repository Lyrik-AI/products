## Where it goes wrong

Most cooking classes start with the ingredients already washed, weighed and waiting on a tray. Which removes the half of Chinese cooking that happens before anyone turns on a flame. So this one starts at the stalls — and ends in a private garden, eating what you made.

![Dried chillies and Sichuan peppercorns](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/5d5511b56e91.webp)

## What you'll come away with, beyond a full stomach

Four hours that start at a Chengdu market stall and end at a table in a private garden, eating the four or five Sichuan dishes you just made at your own wok.

You'll see

- A working Chengdu wet market at trading hour — dried chillies by the sack, live fish, tofu still warm
- How a chef picks: what he rejects, what he pays, what he refuses to pay
- A private garden restaurant most visitors never find the door to

You'll cook and eat

- The best-known Sichuan dishes, made by you at your own wok and burner
- The numbing heat built from scratch — you'll know what makes it
- Everything brought to one table at the end and eaten together

You'll hear

- Why the cut changes the dish, and why some things go into cold oil
- Where the ma la numbness actually comes from
- The chef's own shorthand for judging an ingredient in two seconds

You'll take home

- The written recipes for everything you cooked
- A working sense of how to bargain in a Chinese market
- The ability to make it again with what your own supermarket sells

## How it unfolds

08:30 · Buying is half the skill

The chef joins you at the market. You buy what the class needs, and along the way get the wider picture — how the Chinese cuisines divide up, where Sichuan sits among them, and why the same three ingredients turn into completely different dishes depending on the province. Bargaining is part of it. You will be encouraged to try.

![An aisle of fruit and vegetable stalls in a local market](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/f3ef9ee972a3.webp)

09:30 · One flame each

The class is held at a private home-style garden restaurant rather than a demonstration kitchen, and everyone gets their own station and their own equipment. It sounds like a small detail. It is the difference between cooking and watching someone cook.

![Guests cooking at a row of wok stations in the garden](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/0b5e11990d57.webp)

10:00 · Your wok, your mistakes

You cook the best-known Sichuan dishes yourself, step by step, at your own flame, with the chef walking the line. There is a moment in every session where somebody puts the chilli in too early and the whole garden finds out — and that, genuinely, is when people learn what the sequence is for.

![A guest cooking at a wok station](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/87738d837877.webp)

10:40 · Why that cut, why cold oil

Alongside the cooking there is the explanation: why the cut changes the dish, why some things go into cold oil and some into smoking oil, and where the famous numbing heat actually comes from. Sichuan food has a logic, and once you have it you stop needing recipes for anything that isn't written down.

![A chef guiding a guest through knife work](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/18252cea5bfe.webp)

11:20 · Then you eat what you made

Everything comes to one table and you eat it together, in the garden, comparing whose worked. This is the actual end of the experience rather than a formality tacked onto it — and it is why the class is scheduled for four hours instead of two.

![Guests in aprons with their woks in the garden kitchen](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/e6982f471666.webp)

## You leave with the recipes

The bit that outlasts the day

Almost every cooking class in the world sends you home with a photograph and a full stomach, and by the following month you could not reproduce a single thing you made.

This one hands you the recipes for what you cooked, written to be used in your own kitchen with what you can actually buy — a small thing to include, and the reason people write back months later.

It also runs twice a day, morning and afternoon, which makes it the easiest thing on this list to fit around everything else you are doing in Chengdu.
