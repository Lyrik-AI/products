## Want to climb the Great Wall, but your trip is in Shanghai or Zhejiang ?

The Great Wall is the first wish on a lot of travelers' lists for China. But if your itinerary is in Shanghai or Zhejiang, getting to Badaling means a four-hour-plus high-speed train to Beijing first, and shuffling through holiday crowds once you're there. You don't have to go that far. The Taizhou Prefecture Wall in Linhai, Zhejiang, is known as the “Jiangnan Great Wall”: the Ming general Qi Jiguang fought pirates here, and it was he who built its two-storey hollow watchtowers. When he was posted north later on, the design went with him, onto the Ming Great Wall. The architectural historian Luo Zhewen called this wall the “model” and the “blueprint” for the northern Ming wall. You leave your hotel in the morning, stand on the ramparts in the afternoon, and sleep in your own bed that night.

![The Taizhou Prefecture Wall climbing the ridge of Beigu Hill](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/71bf21c424df.jpg)

## What you get on the Jiangnan Great Wall

Climb for the view, shoot without the crowds, plus a pirate-fighting story and old-street snacks

Stand on the ramparts and look out over river and town

The wall runs along the Ling River on one side and up over Beigu Hill on the other. Climb the Haohan Slope and the rooftops of the old town and the whole river are below you — the “standing on the Great Wall” moment you had in mind.

Skip the crowds

On weekdays this stretch of wall is far quieter than Badaling. No waiting ages for a photo, and you can sit down and rest whenever your legs want a break. Holidays do get busy here, so avoid them if quiet is what you're after.

A real pirate-fighting story

Pirates, a celebrated general, and a curved rampart built to keep out enemies and floods at the same time — your guide talks as you walk, and you'll find out how the watchtowers on the northern Great Wall were learned from here.

An old street that's still lived in

The old shops on Ziyang Street are all still trading. Lunch happens right there on the street: freshly fried egg-white “sheep's tails”, seaweed biscuits hot off the griddle. None of it is a set dressed up for tourists.

## What to see on the wall, what to eat on Ziyang Street

Your guide stops at these spots up on the wall, and picks out a few snacks with you on Ziyang Street.

Signature

![A two-storey hollow watchtower on the Taizhou Prefecture Wall](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/2a904e22ab74.jpg)

Two-storey hollow watchtower

Built under Qi Jiguang's direction, with two levels and room for troops to shelter from the rain. It's the ancestor of the watchtowers on the northern Ming Great Wall — stand inside and you're standing at the beginning of the Great Wall's story.

Signature

![The Taizhou Prefecture Wall and its semicircular barbican beside the Ling River](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/e66fa7940420.jpg)

The barbican and the curved bastion

The side facing the Ling River was deliberately built as a semicircle, so floodwater flows around it. The wall still shields the old town from flooding today.

![Steep stone steps of the wall on Beigu Hill](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/36d776cf5bc6.jpg)

The Beigu Hill stretch and Haohan Slope

The steepest section, and the best one for photos. Climb it and the wall winds along the ridge with the whole old town at your feet.

Signature

![Old shopfronts on Ziyang Street in Linhai](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/c7edbf6ae85f.jpg)

Ziyang Street

More than a kilometre of old street at the foot of the wall, and officially a Chinese Historical and Cultural Street. Egg-white “sheep's tails” are crisp outside and soft within, seaweed biscuits come straight off the griddle, and a shibingtong is one thin crepe rolled full of fillings.

Signature

![Egg-white “sheep's tails” served in a paper bowl](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/78b3dc5dbb47.jpg)

Egg-white “sheep's tail”

A famous Linhai snack: egg whites whipped stiff, wrapped around red bean paste and deep-fried. Crisp outside, soft inside, and named for its resemblance to a sheep's tail.

![A seaweed biscuit broken open](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/6a27c1fcf709.jpg)

Seaweed biscuit

A freshly baked flaky pastry with a seaweed filling — savoury, crisp, and often worth a queue at the old shops on Ziyang Street.

![A freshly fried prawn fritter](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/742e2f615936.jpg)

Fried prawn fritter

Fresh prawns and minced pork in a batter, deep-fried until golden and puffed, and eaten hot as you walk.

![A box of egg-wrapped mochi with sweet-and-sour sauce poured over it](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/93710e23e5f0.jpg)

Sweet-and-sour mochi in egg

Soft glutinous rice cake wrapped in egg and griddled, then finished with a sweet-and-sour sauce. Sweet with a tang.

## Frequently asked questions

Is the Jiangnan Great Wall the Great Wall?

It isn't the Great Wall that runs past Beijing — it's the Taizhou Prefecture Wall in Linhai, Zhejiang, also called the “Jiangnan Great Wall” or “Jiangnan Badaling”. The hollow watchtowers Qi Jiguang built here were later used on the northern Ming Great Wall, which is why this wall is called its “blueprint”.

How long does it take to get to Linhai from Shanghai? Can it be done in a day?

Shanghai Hongqiao to Linhai is about 3 hours by high-speed rail, so a day trip works. On a Shanghai departure we collect you from your hotel around 07:30 and return you around 21:00; from Zhejiang, everything is arranged around your departure point.

Can we start from Zhejiang?

Yes. We depart from either Shanghai or Zhejiang and pick you up at your hotel; train times and departure times are rearranged around where you're starting from, and the times on this page assume a Shanghai departure.

Is the wall hard to climb? Can older travelers and children manage it?

The riverside stretch is fairly gentle, while the Beigu Hill section has steep steps. How far you walk is up to you, and you can stick to the gentle stretch. If anyone in your group has limited mobility, please tell us in advance.

Why does the price per person go down when more people come?

The private car and guide are arranged for the group as a whole, so the more people travelling together, the less each person pays. Three and four people pay the same rate; five or more get the best per-person price.

What language does the guide speak?

English and Chinese guides are provided as standard; German, French, Spanish, Italian and other languages need to be arranged in advance when you book.

What documents do we need to bring?

Please bring your original passport. High-speed rail and the scenic area both require names registered to each traveler, and we book your train tickets and entrance passes in advance using your passport details.
