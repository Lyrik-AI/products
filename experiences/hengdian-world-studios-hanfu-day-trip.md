## Watching a costume drama, have you ever wanted to play a part yourself ?

After “Empresses in the Palace” or “Story of Yanxi Palace”, what stays with you is the phoenix crowns, the dragon robes and the white marble steps you climb one by one; after a wuxia film, you've imagined running along rooftops and drawing a sword. But at the Forbidden City in Beijing you can only look on from behind a barrier — no costumes, no filming, and certainly no starring role. Hengdian is different. It's one of China's largest film and television production bases, nicknamed “Chinawood”, and the Palace of the Ming and Qing Dynasties was built using the Forbidden City as its model — the very place where those Qing court dramas were shot. Today you're not just visiting a film set. You're here to act.

![A group of visitors in period costume laughing together in front of palace steps](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/5056322a0536.jpg)

## Never acted before? That's fine

Hair, makeup, costumes and filming are all handled for you, and you take the finished film home

Professional styling in one go

A makeup artist does your face and hair for the role you've chosen, and your costume is fitted from head to toe. For a lot of people, that first look in the mirror is unrecognisable.

Real palaces and real sets

The red walls, gilded roofs and white marble steps are the places Qing court dramas actually filmed — not a green screen in a studio.

Someone to walk you into the scene

The crew tells you where to walk, when to turn your head, how to draw the sword. Your guide is beside you handling the language, so communication isn't a problem.

Take home a short film starring you

What you shoot becomes your own costume film, ready to send to family and friends or post on social media — a travel memento that's hard to reproduce anywhere else.

## Today, who do you want to be ?

Costumes, hair and makeup and sets are all ready. You just pick a role.

![An emperor in a dragon robe and an elaborately dressed empress seated side by side on the throne](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/e3da52efabfb.jpg)

Emperor and empress

Dragon robe, phoenix crown, throne — the moment you sit down, the whole palace is yours. Good for couples, or for anyone who fancies a turn as “We, the Emperor”.

![A visitor in Qing court dress and banner headdress in front of a red wall](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/7b46d6cb08d3.jpg)

Qing court consort

The banner headdress, the platform shoes, the embroidered court robe — the exact look that stepped straight out of “Story of Yanxi Palace”. Glance back against the red wall, glide down the long corridor.

![A visitor in a swordsman's costume striking a fighting pose on set](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/7c21bf77c05f.jpg)

Wuxia hero

A long robe, a sword. Leap, trade blows, strike the standoff pose before the duel — live out the martial arts dream you've had since childhood.

![A couple in hanfu against a period-style set](https://cdn.lyriktrip.com/s/88cbbf3250b6.jpg)

Hanfu scholar and beauty

Wide sleeves, crossed collar, robes floating as you move, as if stepping out of an old painting. If acting isn't for you, you can still shoot a set of photos and a short film with the most Chinese feel of anything you'll take home.

## Frequently asked questions

I've never acted and I don't speak Chinese. Is that a problem?

Not at all. The crew walks you through where to go and what to do step by step, and your guide is beside you the whole time handling the language. All you have to do is enjoy it.

Can we play the emperor or empress, or shoot something wuxia?

Yes. Qing court dress (emperor, empress, consort), swordsman's costume and hanfu are all available, and you choose the role you want on site.

Is Hengdian's Palace of the Ming and Qing Dynasties the same as the Forbidden City?

No. The Palace of the Ming and Qing Dynasties was built using Beijing's Forbidden City as its model, as a studio base for shooting costume dramas — it isn't a heritage site. Which is exactly why costumes and filming are possible here.

How long does it take to get to Hengdian from Shanghai?

Shanghai Hongqiao to Hengdian Station is about 2.5 hours on a direct high-speed train. On a Shanghai departure we collect you from your hotel around 08:30 and return you after 21:30; from Zhejiang, everything is arranged around your departure point.

Can we start from Zhejiang?

Yes. We depart from either Shanghai or Zhejiang and pick you up at your hotel; train times and departure times are rearranged around where you're starting from, and the times on this page assume a Shanghai departure.

Why does the price per person go down when more people come?

The private car and guide are arranged for the group as a whole, so the more people travelling together, the less each person pays. Three and four people pay the same rate; five or more get the best per-person price.

Can we bring children?

Yes. Children can get into costume and appear on camera too, and plenty of families act out a scene together. Please ask us about child pricing.

Are children welcome?

Yes. Children can also change into costumes and appear in front of the camera, and many families enjoy acting out a scene together. Please contact customer service for information on children’s pricing.
